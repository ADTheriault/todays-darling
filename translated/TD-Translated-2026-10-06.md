## Title: Good times, good people, good things.
## Date: 2026-10-06
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

The third floor of Hobonichi in Kanda is built, if I had to describe it, like a family restaurant. It doesn't matter who's standing or sitting where. Working, chatting, eating, debating, dozing off, none of it draws any complaint as long as it isn't a serious nuisance to others. There are other, quieter floors, so you can just move if you want to. You're free to peer over someone's shoulder or from the side while they work, hold a meeting, or show something off, and in a sense, some meetings are held precisely because people want to be peered at. This is also the floor where snacks people have given us and all sorts of delicious things get lined up on the counter with a cheerful "Help yourselves." We designed it from the very start to be that kind of place.

During so-called working hours, putting on a so-called working face and staring seriously at a computer screen matters far less than talking about chess, which someone's recently gotten into, or recounting the shock of watching "We Are Aliens," or wondering aloud whether a certain Chinese restaurant's summer-only "chilled scallion noodles" has gone back to being shrimp fried rice now that it's October. Thinking and talking about things like that is what's important. Incidentally, I can't eat shrimp, I'm allergic.

The labor and time spent drafting throwaway plans E, F, and G just to show your counterpart the process of effort you've put in. The hours spent pretending to work diligently. Things like that aren't merely a waste, they're an active hindrance. Far more valuable is the time spent talking back and forth, like a bunch of middle schoolers, about what's fun, what's delightful, what's sad, what's lonely. Everyone already does the serious, steady grind. I think the hours of forced "pretending to work" would be better turned into enjoyable play, for the person's own health, for the mood of their colleagues, and for the future of humanity.
