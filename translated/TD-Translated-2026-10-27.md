## Title: A voice grown old, a voice that won't carry
## Date: 2026-10-27
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Age and aging aren't unrelated. There's certainly a connection. But it isn't anything like a neat, direct proportion. Each of us, of course, sinks deeper into old age at our own pace. In my case, too, it's been creeping along, slowly, little by little. Cataloguing every symptom wouldn't make for interesting reading, but there is one thing I find genuinely sad.

My voice. The muscles of the vocal cords lose their elasticity like everything else, so the instrument I use to make sound has undoubtedly grown old. But I was always someone with a naturally big voice. Without my thinking about it much, it carried a long way. It rang, you might say. I knew it had helped me enormously when it came to communicating. I didn't have to try very hard, and my voice reached people, got through to them. Long before I ever had to wrestle with phrasing or how to put my words together, getting that voice to land, near or far, took no effort at all. That made life easy.

But at some point, I can't quite say when, my voice became harder to hear. The sound that comes out, in both volume and quality, is weaker than what I'm trying to say. If it were too strong, I could bring it down and keep it in check. But I can tell for myself that the tone, the volume, the color of it all feel unreliable. And right there, what little confidence I had shrinks a bit further. There are good days and bad days, it's true. But on the whole, I no longer feel that my voice could reach very far.

Compared with all the other signs of aging, people don't talk about this one much, but watching your voice lose its quality and its strength is a lonely thing. There are glasses for the eyes and hearing aids for the ears, but wanting to be heard doesn't mean you can go around with a megaphone. It isn't that I want to be loud. The ideal is to have a big, clear, ringing voice and be able to use it softly. I have karaoke coming up soon, so this is what's been on my mind.
