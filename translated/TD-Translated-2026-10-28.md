## Title: Do you love a good chance? That's a chance too
## Date: 2026-10-28
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

At the "Dojo," our weekly gathering for what we call deadly serious, super-casual chitchat, we got to talking about what a wonderful thing a "chance" is. Everybody loves a chance. I do too. It's a word that's been tossed around in a pretty ordinary way for as long as anyone can remember, and we all use it as if we know exactly what it means, but somehow it feels like more than plain "luck."

What came to mind for me during that conversation was an example from soccer: when a player spots some space, an open patch of the field, and uses it to carry the play forward, you could say he's found a chance. That led us back to something we'd talked about the other day, the idea that "sense is the ability to find chances."

When I looked up "chance" again, I found it described as a word for an "opportunity" or "possibility" to do something, or else for "accident" or "likelihood." Apparently in English people tend to reach for "opportunity" when they mean the hopeful sense, but who cares? If accidents can be found, I'd like to go looking for them too.

Finding a "possibility" means, in other words, finding "hope." Even when you're stuck and can't move, when the losses keep piling up, when everything has felt like one long overcast sky, a sudden gust of wind can sometimes push those dark clouds aside. Noticing that wind is what a "chance" is, isn't it? If you can see even the faintest possibility of trying something, it leads you toward "hope" in a way that staying flattened never will.

Don't we all love a story like that? You and I, in ways big and small, have kept finding this chance and that chance, and that's how we've made it through to the next day, toward the future, haven't we? Come to think of it, that's what Hobonichi has been doing all along. "I want us to be a team that finds chances and makes chances," is where our conversation ended up.

Today's question, no. 085: "Do you remember a chance of some kind?" Think back, and if you'd like, write it up in an email and send it our way.

Thank you for stopping by Hobonichi again today. It's a little late to be saying so, but that song "Give Peace a Chance" really is a good one.
