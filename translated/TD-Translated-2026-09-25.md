## Title: Feeling the unnecessary things
## Date: 2026-09-25
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

"To feel."

To feel something. To feel the heat, to feel the cold, to feel loneliness, to feel joy, to feel friendship, to feel affection, to feel a slight, to feel anger... There are things that nearly everyone would feel, just by being there in that moment, in that place. And there are times when you weren't really feeling much of anything, but someone else nearby is feeling it, and you get pulled along and end up feeling it too. A single person's act of feeling is complex and subtle. If I had to compare it to something, I'd call it a sense of touch.

Sometimes you feel things you didn't need to. The kind of unnecessary things that everyone else overlooks. Honestly, it might have been better not to feel them at all, and sometimes feeling them stirs up a bit of confusion. You could call it a sense of unease, that sort of thing.

And then you find yourself dwelling on that unnecessary thing for a while. Sometimes you keep thinking about it for quite a long time.

Continuing to dwell on this unnecessary thing leads you into thinking about it from all sorts of angles. And once you start turning it over in your mind, that unnecessary thing you first felt can become an entirely new perspective, a fresh way in, and the thought comes together, gets put into action, and grows into what becomes obvious to everyone in the future. Most of what we call good things happen exactly this way.

Let's try applying this idea of the unnecessary thing to all kinds of examples. Sports, creative work, business, policy. You'll find that in everything, there's this same cycle that begins with a sense of unease. Feeling the unnecessary thing, then dwelling on it, then thinking it through from every direction. Everything starts with feeling, out there in the field.

AI has no field, no daily life, so its ability to feel is weak. And whether you have too much experience or too little, your ability to feel weakens all the same.
