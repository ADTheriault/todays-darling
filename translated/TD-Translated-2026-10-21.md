## Title: So much of the world, so much of life, I just let go by
## Date: 2026-10-21
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

You know, when you think about it, it wouldn't be any fun if you could figure everything out. Say you had some psychic power that let you see whatever was coming. Sure, the people who wanted to make use of that power would fawn all over you, but for you yourself, it might not just be dull. It might be downright painful.

With that little preamble out of the way, let me tell you about one of my misses.

It happens more often than you'd think: a gorgeous pitch comes in, a real beauty, and you don't notice a thing. You just stand there and watch it go by. Strike one.

Lately, the perfect example is "Chiikawa." I'd taken the phrase "some small, cute little thing" and stuffed the whole business inside it, and let it sail right past me. I must have seen it all over the place, and I don't remember ever saying I disliked it, but still, I let it go by.

Looking back, I did feel something like "huh, that's kind of interesting" about the fact that their weapon of choice is a sasumata, one of those forked poles used to pin down intruders. Every so often a one-page strip would turn up on Twitter, and right around the time I started thinking, "I can't quite see the whole picture, but this is a little strange, and funny," I learned where this character called Momonga came from. If I remember right, I'd heard that my daughter's daughter was apparently fond of Momonga, so I looked up what sort of character it was. Once I knew that backstory, and a little corner of the Chiikawa world, my curiosity began to boil over. "What on earth is this?!" I started reading the collected volumes, and then along came the movie.

To longtime fans, I'm the guy who shows up and makes them say, "Now? Seriously?" You didn't know about Nagano's blazing fastball, or those curveballs that break seven different ways? No, I didn't. Or rather, I let them go by.

Come to think of it, there's a lot of this. There's a certain neighborhood Chinese place I'd only known by name and let go by. When it comes to rice varieties, or how to cook it, I've let practically everything go by. And I suspect there are plenty of fine things around the office that I've let go by, too. I'm afraid, dear reader, that I am by no means all-seeing, nor in any way what you'd call a discerning eye.
