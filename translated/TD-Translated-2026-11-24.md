## Title: On things that are touchingly earnest
## Date: 2026-11-24
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

There are all kinds of stories and images out there, but the ones I'm weakest against are the ones that strike me as touchingly earnest. The feelings a dog turns toward another dog, another animal, or its owner. A small child trying with everything they have to do something. A young person, their strength not yet fully grown in, giving it all they've got.

These things come across better with hardly any words. If someone did something touchingly earnest while narrating it, "Here I am, small and weak, still bravely trying," I might find myself a little put off, even if it were a dog or a baby.

I suspect we pick up on this quality more easily when the one doing the trying is small and cute and weak, but it lives in the actions of the rough and the strong, too. Either way, you can't help wanting to cheer them on: don't give up, keep going.

Meanwhile, the me who's feeling all this isn't actually lending a hand. I'm only letting my heart be moved, the way I would by a film or a story. I'm not doing anything that helps. "Earnestness like that is a wonderful thing," I think, and maybe, like a believer gazing up at a holy statue, I'm really just making sure of what lies at the very center of my own heart.

We can't really become touchingly earnest anymore. Puppies can, babies can, most weak things can, but it seems unlikely that we ever will, from here on. One reason is that we can talk, and talk far too much. Whether or not we say things well, we simply have too many words. Another is that we can't keep pushing past our limits forever. Faced with things that don't work out, with efforts that go unrewarded, we've learned how to give up, how to decide it's all a bit ridiculous. People like us probably can't be touchingly earnest anymore. And yet there's plenty that people like us can do precisely because of what we are, and taking on those roles matters too, you know.
