## Title: Things I'm really bad at.
## Date: 2026-08-31
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

There's such a thing as "the correct form."

When you visit a shrine, it's two bows, two claps, one more bow, wasn't it? Even now I can't properly remember where and how you're supposed to do the bows and the final bow.

The same goes for offering incense at a funeral. Which way to face, whom to bow to, what exactly to do. I've done it countless times, and still I don't understand it. I forget what the "correct form" is even supposed to be.

It's not as though anyone's going to scold me for it, but I do wish I could manage it in the "correct form," just like everyone else. And yet I never have the slightest confidence that I actually can.

Whether it's paying respects at a shrine or offering incense, I think what matters is the reverence, toward the enshrined gods or the buddhas, toward the person who has passed away. But the moment I'm in a situation where the "correct form" is fixed and prescribed, my heart goes somewhere else entirely.

I have a feeling this is something I'll carry with me my whole life, never cured. I even suspect I'll manage to bungle something at my own funeral.

When you try to live by drawing closer to the "correct form," you end up asking, "How does everyone else do it?" You look into things like "What's the general practice?" and "What's the going standard?" and "What's the way to do it without embarrassing yourself?" and then you move yourself to fit the answers. Which means your will, your confidence, your judgment, all of it, just disappears.

Lately, watching the people of the world, I notice there are an awful lot of them living their lives with the "correct form" as their axis. When that happens, "the way that can't go wrong" becomes the best option. And so, well, everyone ends up doing their version of two bows, two claps, one bow, and somehow it all feels like "job done."

Let's get back to remembering what actually matters. Being like me, losing myself at weddings and funerals and every ceremonial occasion, that won't do.
