## Title: As I kept writing, it took a turn
## Date: 2026-11-07
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Children are fools. Or rather, children do foolish things. This is a theory I arrived at through my own experience, but it has only grown sturdier the more I've heard other people's stories.

If you jump off something high while holding an open umbrella, you'll float gently down and won't get hurt. Most kids (mostly boys, I'd guess) must have believed this right up until they tried it. Still, one attempt is all it takes to reach the correct conclusion.

They collect things with no idea what they're collecting them for. Pill bugs, bagworms, some kind of trading card, stickers, whatever it is, they set about collecting it. However many years they keep at it, at some point they've simply stopped. Nobody knows why they stopped, but then nobody knows why they started, either. What you come to realize is that the very idea of a reason doesn't exist for them.

As a rule, big things are better and small things are worse. That's the verdict, and it gets applied in every situation imaginable. "Huge!" is a compliment, and in most situations they'd like to be huge themselves.

As I slowly dredge up memories like these, there's something I can't help noticing, whether I like it or not. I started out talking about children, but adults, even if they dip it all in batter and deep-fry it, are pretty much the same, aren't they? Adults are fools too. Or rather, adults do foolish things too.

It isn't only an adult living inside an adult. Some unchanging child ingredient is mixed in there, living on inside the adult-shaped container. And that brings good things, bad things, and awkward things. Just because you've grown up doesn't mean you get to go around acting clever.
