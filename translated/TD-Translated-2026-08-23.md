## Title: On prose and the act of writing
## Date: 2026-08-23
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Specifications, instruction manuals, work memos, the so-called "practical texts" of the world, all aim to convey what needs conveying as accurately as possible. "September 1st (Tuesday) is finally the release date for the Hobonichi Techo 2027," that sort of thing belongs to this category. If anything, that one word, "finally," carries just a little bit of "the feelings of the person who wrote it."

Not everything written in the world is a practical text. There are things that hold the "self" of the writer. You don't need to strike a grand pose and call it "literature." Just as most ordinary chatter does, it's simply something a person wants to say, expressed toward another person. Even "Man, I'm sad" is a form of self-expression.

"I want everyone in the world to understand" is impossible. But then again, "it's fine if only the people who get it get it" won't do either.

Being understood is a bit like a prize, and as you keep winning prizes, your writing gets better. But then, that leads to a new problem, because you start writing with the prize in mind. Honestly, I suspect the best thing would be to stop worrying about it, about understanding or being understood.

To put it very crudely, writing can be divided into two kinds: the "instruction manual" and the "song." Every piece of writing is a blend of the two. What I'm writing right now is one such blend, and this thing I've been going on about lately, "subjectivity," belongs to the "song" side of things. Take it away, and everything turns into nothing but instruction manuals.

◆ The daily interviews. I read tons of them every day, you know. So today, here's this one. No. 019, "Where were you born?" It's a question I have no memory of myself, but how about it? If you'd like, write it in an email and send it my way.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today. The times we're born and the times we die, those are the things we ourselves never get to know.
