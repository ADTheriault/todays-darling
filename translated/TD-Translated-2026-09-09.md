## Title: I shall now excuse myself to the restroom.
## Date: 2026-09-09
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

This year's "Let's Play Baseball" has come to an end. Some years you head home happy, other years you head home swallowing something back. That's just how it goes.

Anyway, on the "radio broadcast" you can only hear inside the ballpark, there was something I really wanted to say, and I wanted the folks at the commentary desk to agree with me too, so I said it.

"There's this unwritten rule that during a broadcast, neither the announcer nor the commentator is allowed to use the bathroom, and most people talk about that suffering as if it were some cherished memory, but isn't that just not right? To put it strongly, isn't it a bit of a disregard for basic human rights? Of course, doing your best to hold out is the proper attitude for a professional, I understand that, but come on, let's just go. If you need to go, then go. Fundamentally, nothing bad is going to happen. Even if a baseball broadcast went ten minutes without its commentator, even if a staff member near the announcer had to step in and read out the necessary information at the mic, that's not some disaster, and it's hardly an inconvenience. Throughout the history of broadcasting, the desperate struggle to hold it in has been told as a touching anecdote or a source of laughs, but here at our happily slapdash broadcast booth today, let's all feel free to go to the bathroom. I'm sure I'll be going myself."

That was the gist of what I wanted to say.

Soh Taguchi, who has mountains of experience as a baseball commentator, laughed and said, "If you actually did that, it'd be unprecedented."

Well, in the end, though, nobody actually went to the bathroom. There was no particular need to go when nobody especially wanted to, so that was perfectly fine, but if a true professional like Taguchi, or Kato from Sports Hochi, someone from that "you must not go" world had actually gone, it might have made a small article in the sports papers. Probably, for people involved in long sports broadcasts, not going to the bathroom out of a sense of duty was a little bit of a touching tale. But those kinds of touching tales don't really move anyone anymore. The players themselves go to the bathroom and then play the game, after all.
