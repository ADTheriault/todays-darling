## Title: If a diary had readers, this is how it would be written
## Date: 2026-10-12
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

The middle day of the three-day weekend was the day the Giants' season came to an end. Two losses, and there's no going any further. In other words, it's over. I wasn't predicting it, exactly, but somewhere in the back of my mind I'd had the feeling it wouldn't be surprising if things turned out that way.

What really darkened my mood, though, was how many people were saying vicious things on social media. I suppose they, too, count as "fans." But does being a "fan" mean you get to say whatever you want? Blaming and blaming, raging with the benefit of hindsight, tearing down someone's very character. Watching people scold flesh-and-blood human beings like that, I thought: I would never, ever want to work under someone like this.

If you told me that this is the whole point of watching sports, spewing out whatever you feel like and blowing off the frustrations of everyday life, I'd have nothing to say back. Still, that's what I thought: "I wouldn't want to be one of their subordinates." When someone loses, when their numbers are bad and they just can't get going, do they really deserve to be torn into like that? Hmm? Does this also mean "I wouldn't want them as my subordinates, either"? Maybe it does. A perfectly good team would stop working. It reminds me that when it comes to how we carry ourselves through bad stretches, I need to take a good, hard look at myself, too.

The other day I wrote that I might want to rethink my "smartphone dependence" a little. I've been keeping a bit of distance from my iPhone. In its place, it seems, I'm spending more time on my MacBook. And the me who's "dependent on his phone," well, that's right, I shouldn't be blaming him either. I'll sit down with myself, with some affection, and talk over how to go about this.

I bought a pair of barefoot shoes (the kind meant to feel like walking barefoot). When I walk, the soles of my feet feel every bump, but I'll probably get used to it. At the very least, just starting to wear them has satisfied my curiosity. Where it goes from here, I have no idea. I'm not at the point of recommending them to anyone yet, but I'm sure I'll report back sometime on how it's going.
