## Title: My own secret theory. But it's wrong.
## Date: 2026-08-20
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

There's an idea I keep to myself, one I'd never recommend to anyone else, but that I nonetheless "use." The idea is wrong. I know that. And one of these ideas is my theory of "fifty-fifty odds."

When you roll a die, hoping for a particular number, the chance of it coming up is one in six. Roll two dice and things get a little more complicated: seven has six ways of appearing, making it the most likely, while two and twelve each have only one way, so their odds are low. And yet, if there's a number I'm hoping for, say "three," then the outcome is simply this: either "three" comes up, or it doesn't.

It either appears or it doesn't, one of two possibilities, and so I decide, all on my own, that this makes it "fifty-fifty odds." You can see that this "theory (?)" is mistaken. Even so, as I rattle the dice in my hand, right up until the moment I let them go, I chant to myself: "fifty-fifty." Sometimes I miss. Sometimes I hit.

And this way of thinking, I actually "use" it (quietly, privately) in situations well beyond rolling dice. There's a kind of randomness, a not-knowing, much like a roll of the dice, that turns up in all sorts of moments in life. When that happens, quite apart from the version of me that's talking things over with everyone, I try to see the outcome as one of two things: either "it hits" or "it doesn't."

If it hits, I move on to whatever comes next. If it misses, I think about what to do next anyway. So either way, nothing ends right there on the spot. It becomes "Okay. On to the next one." What scares me more than anything is losing heart somewhere in that process. Eventually, the "three" or the "four" I was hoping for does come up.

In the truly important moments, I don't actually say any of this out loud. This isn't a matter of science. It's a matter of how you hold your heart.
