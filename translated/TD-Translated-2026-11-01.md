## Title: What people are capable of, for better or worse
## Date: 2026-11-01
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

There are things I think I understand in my head, and yet, if I'm honest, I somehow still don't really get them. Take "personal information," which comes up so often these days. I haven't quite sorted out what I think about it. Your name and address, plus your phone number (or these days, I suppose, your email address), count as important "personal information." That much I understand perfectly well. Naturally, it would be a problem if they were used by bad people.

But when you have a pizza or some takeout delivered, they know your phone number, your address, your name, and very nearly your face. Maybe you sometimes order under a fake name, and maybe the handoff can happen without anyone seeing anyone, but on the whole it's normal to assume they know who you are. The person who puts letters in your mailbox can deliver them precisely because they know your name and address. It's not a pleasant way to put it, but your "personal information," and mine, isn't known only to the people we know.

And, speaking strictly in terms of logic, if someone you know were to become an "enemy," your "personal information" would remain in the hands of someone you'd rather didn't have it. If I may push the logic a little further: however close a company comes to perfect security, if a person who works near some crucial secret decides to leak it, that "information" can no longer be protected, just like that.

This is a very TV-drama sort of example, but if there were information you truly wanted to steal, one method might even be to become the person that company or organization needs most, and simply get yourself inside. At the level of nations and defense, I suppose that's what spies do.

I'm someone who would rather not believe that people are bad by nature, but logically speaking, human beings carry a potential, for better or worse, that can rival the failure of any vast system. Yes, "for better or worse," people are magnificent, and terrifying. And yet trying to live without trusting people is also very, very hard.
