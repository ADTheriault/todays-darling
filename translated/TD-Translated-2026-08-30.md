## Title: On the eyes of the Chiikawa characters, and such.
## Date: 2026-08-30
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

So I've been doing what you might call "Chiikawa calligraphy practice," copying the drawings, and there are a few things I've started to understand.

The first thing you learn is that the eyes are difficult. And because they're difficult, you find yourself studying exactly how Nagano, the creator, draws them. At the crudest level, you draw a circle and put a single line down the middle, like a grain of barley. Or so I thought, but it turns out that's not quite it. For the small drawings, yes, they're done that way, but if you look closely, there's another circle inside the first circle. And the bottom part of the big circle becomes a smiling crescent moon. It's a bit like drawing the number 9 inside the circle of the eye. What matters is that both the large circle and the small circle mustn't warp or come to a point. They have to be gently, calmly round. The smiling-moon part at the bottom can get smaller, but you have to be careful not to fill it in solid black (all of which you may well have skimmed right past).

And even once you've grasped roughly this much, you still can't actually draw it that way, so the day grows dark and the road stretches on. What's more, Chiikawa, Usagi, and Hachiware, all three of them look alike, but the eyes are drawn just a little differently for each. On top of that there's the placement of the eyes and mouth, the position of the ears, the way the eyebrows are drawn distinctly, the thickness of the lines, their momentum, their sense of speed. It's a formidable thing.

But since it's been made into an animation, there must be more than just the creator's own subtle touch at work. There has to be a set of "rules for drawing Chiikawa" that lets the drawing staff reproduce it, at least to some degree. I can't stop wishing I could somehow arrive at those rules. It would be wonderful if we could hold a "Chiikawa calligraphy class" at Hobonichi's school. I wonder if we could pull it off. Anyway, yesterday too I spent the whole day thinking about nothing but "Chiikawa and AI."

The movie tells the story from volume 8 of the manga, but if you want to grasp the outline of this world, you'd do well to read volume 1.
