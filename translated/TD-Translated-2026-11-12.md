## Title: Trivial things not even worth writing about
## Date: 2026-11-12
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Give a piece the title "Trivial things not even worth writing about," and suddenly whatever you write becomes material. This is nothing more than a passing whim, but let's give it a go.

First, I wake to my iPhone alarm. It's Koji Tamaki's "Melody," and I scramble to shut it off the moment the opening words come in. Once I'm up, I sync the Oura Ring I wore overnight and check how I slept. Usually, not all that well. My sleep score is in the 70s on a good night and the 50s on a bad one, and I'm forever wishing I could pay back this thing they call sleep debt.

I take my wake-up pills with 50cc of water from the kettle. I fold the duvet in half and tidy it away. I head to the washroom, into the toilet, out again. I strip down to my underpants and weigh myself. 63 kilos. Even when it changes, it's almost always within a kilo. I don't use the word "almost" much in my writing. The "almost" in Hobonichi, whose name means "almost daily," already puts me over my maximum allowance.

I take out the mouthguard I wear because I grind my teeth. I brush, currently with a Doltz sonic toothbrush. Rather than loungewear, I get about halfway into the clothes I'll go out in. Right before I actually head out the door, I'll reconsider and add a few things.

Vegetable juice, at the moment whatever comes in a carton. Into the shaker go protein powder, dextrin, Calpis and soy milk, and I shake away while looking over the sports paper. Certain kinds of information I read with obsessive thoroughness. I glance through the print edition of the Nikkei too, but try not to read too much of it. If my "friend at home" asks me something, I'll tell her whether I'd like bread or rice; if she isn't around or doesn't ask, I figure something out myself.

Good grief, that's already about thirty minutes. All of the above happens in nearly exactly the same way every day. It's a bit like Ichiro's routine at the plate. Truly, a morning with nothing particularly happy in it, but nothing unhappy either.
