## Title: Adults have "feelings" too.
## Date: 2026-08-02
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

"You only understand your parents' devotion once you have children of your own." Well, it really is true, isn't it. You could swap in all sorts of other positions and it would still hold. "You only understand your teacher's kindness once you have students," or "You only understand your senior's kindness once you have juniors." I've done a lot of things over a long time now, so all I can really say is, "Sorry, truly, for everything."

The ones who learn something first, who figure it out first, teach it and pass it on to the ones who come after. And the ones who come along later to learn it, well, they don't feel especially grateful. They just take the teaching for granted. It's probably something close to instinct. The monkeys up on Monkey Mountain do it, dogs and cats do it. And humans, too, keep repeating this same pattern, quite naturally.

Say "a parent's devotion" and it starts to sound like some moral lesson, but there's a gentler way to put it: "a parent's feelings." Why did my own parents do the things they did, say the things they said? Once you become a parent yourself, these things start to make sense.

During summer vacation in elementary school, we had radio calisthenics in the schoolyard. I imagine they still do it in places that still do it. I'd hang the little card around my neck, the one you got stamped each day, and head off, grumbling to myself about how much of a pain it was to get up so early. I'd just sort of drift over to the school with the other kids from the neighborhood.

Then one day, my father, who was always hungover and never a morning person, announced, "Radio calisthenics are good for your health, so I'm coming too," and off he went to the school with me. I was a little embarrassed to go with a parent, but there was also a tiny part of me that felt happy about it, and so the two of us walked together to Momoi Elementary for the morning exercises.

That's the whole memory, nothing more, but now I understand it. It wasn't really "because it's good for your health." He simply wanted to tag along and do the calisthenics with his elementary-school kid. If there was a chance to be with your child, you wanted to be with them. That was the feeling behind it, I now see. And I understand it because I'm the same way myself.

Thank you for coming to Hobonichi today. There's also the saying, "A parent's heart, unknown to the child," and I rather like that one too.
