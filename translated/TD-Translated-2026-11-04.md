## Title: Staff meals, home cooking, and the pleasures of everyday life
## Date: 2026-11-04
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Of course there's nothing wrong with the "supreme flavors" of famous, wildly acclaimed restaurants, or with places boasting however many Michelin stars. But what I really want to eat is the staff meals and home cooking of the people who make that kind of food. I know plenty of them will say, "Oh, I don't cook at home," and if that's the case, then I'd like to try whatever good little place in the neighborhood they love.

This idea has been pretty fundamental to me ever since I started Hobonichi. When I launched a series with Takaaki Yoshimoto, a thinker known for being notoriously difficult, I even titled it "Takaaki Yoshimoto's Staff Meals." Not a showpiece made with expensive ingredients and no effort spared, but the everyday food someone sets in front of you with a casual "Stay and eat." That's the kind of thing you can keep close company with for a long time. Sure, the high-end, the lavish, the delicate, the supreme, the ultimate, they're all fine. But somehow, what I look forward to is "the usual."

Recently I came across a manga series called Nishimochi's Everyday. Apart from the work the artist produces as a professional cartoonist, there's this one extra line devoted to drawing daily life. It's done diary-style, in simple lines, and yet reading it feels like having a pleasant conversation with someone you're close to. Yes, it's exactly that: a staff meal, home cooking.

Come to think of it, Yuko Higuchi's Boris Picture Diary, a loose, easygoing four-panel strip about everyday life, was wonderful too, and Ayako Ishiguro's Tenmaru and Family Picture Diary was genuinely funny and I adored it. But both of them stopped at a certain point. I suspect maybe they quit "before it turned into real work," but as a reader, I'd have happily kept reading them forever. Work that's made lightly, almost offhandedly, like home cooking, with no intention of impressing anyone: I like it so much that I'd almost say I prefer it.

Today's question, no. 092: "What's your favorite snack, or your favorite thing to nibble on with a drink?" Give it a little thought. If you'd like, write it in an email and send it our way.

Thank you for coming by Hobonichi again today. Now that I think of it, our Everyday Pleasures Fair might be something along the same lines.
