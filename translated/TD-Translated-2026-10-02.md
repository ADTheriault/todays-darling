## Title: Wandering about, another day just like any other.
## Date: 2026-10-02
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Yesterday, in something of a muddle, I wrote that October 1st was going to be "quite a day," and somehow or other I made it through. Most of the days I'd dreaded, the ones where I kept thinking "I'm scared of when this day comes," end up becoming "yesterday" one way or another. That's just how it goes. "Shigesato Itoi's Live Reading" really was hard work, but it was the kind of hard work that felt genuinely worthwhile. Above all, I enjoyed it myself. I suppose what I'm doing is simply taking what I normally do silently, alone, in my head, and letting myself have fun with it out loud. I probably won't stop just yet. I'm thinking about a third book.

I'd left my smartphone behind and was out when I suddenly remembered something I absolutely had to get in touch about. If I went home or back to the office it would sort itself out, but I couldn't wait that long, so I was getting a little frantic. Wasn't there one of those old red public phones somewhere? I looked, but of course there was no such thing. And yet, by some stroke of luck, there was a phone booth. Surely the phone inside wouldn't have been removed? Oh good, it was fine, there it was, an actual phone. No phone card in my wallet, but I had a ten-yen coin. All I had to do was make the call, and up to that point everything was fine. But then it hit me. I didn't know the number. If it's saved in your phone, you can call without knowing the number at all. Of course. I never knew the numbers in the first place. And yet somehow I used to make calls, used to send messages and all the rest. Come to think of it, the only phone number I have memorized these days is my home number, the one you write down when applying for something. It's quite possible I don't know a single other one. The only thing that comes to mind is that old jingle, the "Castella is number one, the phone is number two" song. And even then, dialing just "two" would hardly get you through to Bunmeido.

It reminded me of the time my electric toothbrush ran out of battery, and until it finally dawned on me that I could just brush by hand, I stood there frozen, thinking, oh no, what do I do. Convenience can shade into inconvenience, I suppose, but well, there's no helping it.
