## Title: What does the real me like, and what do I want to do?
## Date: 2026-08-04
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Some insects and animals, it seems, use tools. Nothing terribly elaborate, but they manage things their "born-and-raised-as-is" bodies couldn't do on their own, using a twig here, a torn-off leaf there. Monkeys don't make pottery or stone tools, but surely they'll pick something up off the ground and put it to use. Even that much is no small thing.

Human beings, on the other hand, live buried in tools. To move, to cook, to play, to make things, and beyond that, we even make tools for making tools. Add to that the ability to think using language, and we've come to feel as though there's nothing we can't do. Think it up, and you can build the very thing you thought of. That's astonishing. At this point, no monkey or crow could ever catch up to us.

And now that "generative AI" has entered the picture too, we're drifting into an age where we might forget what humans could even do in the first place.

But we mustn't forget, and I don't want to forget. Just how much can a person do with nothing but their own flesh and bone? We lean so completely on tools and machines to get through life, but how much can we really do with our own two hands? How far could we get by drawing on everything we've learned and everything we've done up to now? (Maybe that's the sort of thing they explore in sports.) What could we do if we couldn't borrow anyone else's help? What is it we would absolutely refuse to do? If there were something we wanted to protect no matter what, what would it be? And what are the things we truly love?

Normally nobody asks us these things, and there's no particular reason anyone would. But I think those questions, and their answers, and the process of thinking them through, are precisely what make up "me, and no one else."

Tools and machines have come to do everything for us, but "what do I want done?" and "how do I want to be?" are still things that "I, and no one else" get to decide.

Before I forget it all, I want to remember.
