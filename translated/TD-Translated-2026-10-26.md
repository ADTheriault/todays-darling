## Title: Don't bully an AI, or a loofah, or an octopus, for that matter
## Date: 2026-10-26
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Apparently there's such a thing as "bullying AI." I suppose it takes all sorts of forms: hurling the kind of abuse you could never say to another human being, doing something nasty in the middle of an ongoing conversation, feeding it false information, and so on. The AI, presumably, doesn't get "hurt" by any of this, and if you asked it, "Does that hurt you?", the AI itself would tell you, "I don't experience being hurt." The flip side of bullying AI is thanking it, but apparently that doesn't make it "happy" either. Some people even say that just trading thank-yous is a waste of energy resources.

I'm not trying to play the good guy here, but I don't want to bully AI, and I don't intend to. And while it may not be the correct thing to do, I say thank you to AI, and I'll keep on saying it. You might be thinking, what's the point of that? But for me there's meaning in it, and purpose, and reason, and conviction, and even a certain pleasure. I can explain it in my own way, so let me give it a try.

Put as simply as I can, it comes down to this. It's the same with a dog or a cat: doing that sort of thing dirties your own soul. Even if the other side isn't listening, the soul of the one doing the talking gets dirty. You become the soul of someone who says things like that. And a soul like that starts living that way. I don't want that, so I don't want to do it. That's all there is to it.

To put it in slightly more scientific-sounding terms, if you apply a force to something (action), the same force ought to come back at you (reaction). Whether it's a person, a dog, a cat, a wall, or an AI, every action has its reaction. That's my explanation. It's an image I find myself using quite a lot. Like how the bill for whatever you've done usually shows up a little late, you know?
