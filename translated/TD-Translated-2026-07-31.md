## Title: The one cornering you might be yourself.
## Date: 2026-07-31
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

It's not really my place to say this, being the half-hearted coward that I am (and I've packed in more kanji than usual here), but I suspect that in a good many of the cases where we tell ourselves "there's no escape" or "I have no choice," escape is actually possible. It would probably be more persuasive if I went through concrete examples one by one, but that's a hassle, so I won't. And blithely saying "that's a hassle, so I won't" is itself a way of running from the trouble of taking the trouble.

Maybe, to get through to more people in a way they'd really understand, I ought to offer some real examples. But even as I think that might be true, I wonder: would doing so actually get the point across all that well?

Take the old saying, "even a dog that walks will bump into a stick." It isn't concrete communication in the slightest. Some people will think there's no use listening to a story about a dog. Others will want to say, "So what?" And yet that abstract, seemingly empty phrase can suddenly come back to a person and set them walking again.

Now, let me return to that first "there's no escape." If, in roughly the same offhand way as the dog and the stick, you happen to remember that some half-hearted coward once said, "situations you think you can't escape, that you have no choice about, are actually pretty escapable," it just might be that the memory comes alive and starts to move. I suppose I half believe in that, which is why I'm sitting here writing this meddlesome little piece.

You could shorten it to: "You can get out of things more easily than you'd think." I've said it before in another form, too: "People don't actually do the things they truly hate." Often enough, you're only tied down by ropes you've wrapped around yourself.

Today, some volunteers from Hobonichi are running through the scorching heat of Tokyo. They're carrying a delivery in a relay all the way to Tadanori Yokoo's place. "Make your peace with the heat," I offered, by way of encouragement.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today. Freedom is something we go and sell off of our own accord.
