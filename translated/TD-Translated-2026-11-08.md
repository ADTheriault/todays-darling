## Title: Here I am, at my age, still saying things like this
## Date: 2026-11-08
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I started something I call "Chiikawa rinsho," copying Chiikawa drawings the way calligraphy students copy from a master's model, and I still haven't quit. Nobody has actually told me, "You'll never stick with it." But I'm sure somebody would, so from the very start I've been stubbornly refusing to quit (ha). Well, if I'm honest, the one most likely to say it is me. But it's still going, you hear that, self?

Doing something every day is genuinely fascinating. When you repeat more or less the same thing over and over, the "extra thinking," the quibbling and the excuses, starts to fall away. That alone makes it good training for me.

Rinsho doesn't ask you to be original in the first place. Some individuality is bound to slip through, but the idea is simply to write it the way the model shows you. Chiikawa rinsho is drawing rather than lettering, but it's the same in that you're following the path a brush or pen traveled. Someone like me, though, short on both spirit and skill, can't actually follow that movement. It's probably like copying your dance teacher's moves and still not really dancing. I'm supposedly looking right at the model, yet I'm not seeing it. I suppose I could just trace where the lines and dots fall, but then the motion, the thing that actually happened there, would freeze in place and die. And since I don't have the posture or the technique for that motion, I can't move the brush or pen the way I mean to. Dots, circles, straight lines: I can't draw a single one of them properly.

Having said all that, I'm not discouraged. I'm drawing again today. I post them on Twitter like I'm handing in homework, and some people kindly tell me, "You're getting better!" To which I say: oh no, not at all, but I'm not stopping. And once again it hits me how wonderful the teacher who drew the originals really is.
