## Title: Some thoughts I had over the weekend
## Date: 2026-10-04
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

So the Hanshin Tigers clinched the pennant, and with that, the baseball season has finally wrapped up. What a season it was. All sorts of things happened. The Giants' manager suddenly stepped down one day, and the man who was told, "You take over," well, that's no small thing to be handed. You can't exactly sit back and say something modest like, "Oh, someone like me couldn't possibly..." Not when it's professional baseball, where "fighting spirit" is the whole point. It must have been rough. In a way, it was a year of rebuilding. Day after day, it was about rising up from a tough position and figuring out how to see the fight through to the end. And to still be in the running for the title late in the season, I'd say that's more than enough to be proud of. Of course, if you start wanting more, there's no end to it.

I went to see "We Are Aliens" for the second time. I almost never used to watch a movie more than once, but maybe "Chiikawa" got me into the habit. Maybe I just want to take in all that painstaking handiwork, the way you can tell the people who made it went to such lengths, right down to the smallest details. And the second viewing was good again. It really is something. The enthusiasm, the care, the sense of taste, all of it is left behind as expression in every corner of the film. Maybe going to see it again felt like going on a hunt to discover all of that. Like spotting an ammonite fossil in a marble wall. And let me tell you, this movie is absolutely crawling with ammonites. And in a larger sense, I think it's a story of "affirmation." Remember how there used to be a movie tagline that went, "Live."? I almost want to borrow it again right here. That's the feeling it left me with. By the final scene, I actually ended up in tears.

People are free to think whatever they think, and to say whatever they say. But because they're free, they also end up thinking and saying cruel things. Once, something like that would have been born and vanished like a bubble, tucked away in some corner where no one would ever see it. But these days, even graffiti in a back alley is a form of transmission that other people can see. Lies, slander, foul-mouthed insults, they all go flying off into the air. And without even realizing it, we breathe them in too.
