## Title: More about the "100 questions interview"
## Date: 2026-08-08
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

We've only just started, but this "Interview of 100 Questions," the one I'm putting to those of you reading this, is turning out to be quite fascinating.

I began it in a light spirit, thinking of it as a kind of "daily bonus," but it might just become something deeper than that. Over a hundred days, the accumulated thoughts of a fair number of people could amount to something like "a memory of an era, and of human beings." Perhaps no one has ever attempted, or recorded, anything quite like it before.

These days, you're always being pressed to answer right away, aren't you? "Which is it?" Left or right, friend or foe, like it or hate it, answer now. But this is nothing like that. There's no correct answer, no objective, no survey being conducted. It's simply time spent by a great many people "thinking about something." That's what I proposed we record.

Honestly, it's perfectly fine if you forget what you thought afterward. I did say to write in and tell me by email, but there's no better or worse, no skilled or clumsy here. "Everyone's is good." I just felt that if, here and there, the questions became a chance for people to pause for a moment and "ask themselves something," then that would be enough.

Because think about it, take a question like "What's your favorite flower?" Unless someone asks you outright, you rarely stop to consider it, right? By asking yourself and "turning it over in your mind," you end up discovering yourself anew: "Oh, so I'm someone who loves camellias." That sort of thing.

The truth is, I'm thinking through my own answers too, even as I'm the one asking. Someday I may reveal them, but I also feel it's my own freedom to keep some of them to myself forever.

In that sense, I don't think you should treat your answers as something "for the email." Sometimes you can keep a secret a secret, and simply write it down in a notebook that belongs to you alone.

◆Now then, let's carry on with today's interview.

No. 004: "What sort of place do you live in?"

First, think about it freely, and jot it down. And if you feel like it, write it in an email and send it my way.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today. If we keep this up for a hundred rounds, I'm sure "your very own book" will take shape.
