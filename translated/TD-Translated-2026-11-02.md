## Title: Losing, losing, losing, losing, and winning now and then?
## Date: 2026-11-02
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I can't say everyone has it, but it seems like an awful lot of people do. It isn't an instinct anymore, and yet it's a desire that comes very close to one. I think of it as the hunger to win. People just want to win, plain and simple. Or maybe what it really comes down to is that they can't stand to stay beaten. Even in some trivial argument, people try to win, and they mean it. It's the kind of thing people tease each other about, calling it "lose-and-you-die syndrome."

A lot of the time it isn't even about ourselves. We root for an athlete or a team and want them to win. We want it desperately, as if we'd come apart if they lost. The things we own, the place we were born: there too, we simply want to win. Once it's between countries, or religion or ideology gets mixed in, that wanting-to-win gets tangled up in conflicts as well. And people who come to grief through gambling probably started out the same way, with "I can't stay beaten" or "I want to win," until they reached a point where they couldn't back out.

In ordinary life, people don't actually win very much (even sumo wrestlers don't win every bout). If anything, we're forever aware of having lost, or of not measuring up. So we need to be winning at something just to keep ourselves going. It can be sports, it can be your favorite idol hitting it big, it can be a big payout at the horse races, it can be your son's Little League game. Somewhere, at something, we want to win so that things balance out. I think that's what's going on (this is a theory I came to by watching myself, mind you). Since those of us out here in the world are mostly not winning, maybe we're buying our wins, spending money and so on to get them. That said, I don't know whether it works the same way for women.
