## Title: There's this thing called the Hobonichi dojo.
## Date: 2026-09-15
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

A few years back, we started up a "dojo" at Hobonichi. It's not as though we've taken up judo or kendo or anything like that. Though, well, I am practicing tug-of-war pretty hard at the moment.

The "dojo" is a place where you throw around opinions and ideas, where you get thrown and knocked down yourself. Regardless of age or job or career, you toss out all sorts of themes and just talk. It's a bit like free sparring, I suppose. You grapple, no matter what. The senior people aren't necessarily the more skilled ones, but it's also a time when the newcomers get to be present, watching people with a certain amount of experience throw each other around. What matters, above all, is sharing your thoughts and questions.

I once heard that if you ask why the powerhouse teams in sports are so strong, it comes down to the environment of "always being surrounded by strong people," and that this is what nurtures everyone there.

You take a problem that has no easy answer, and each of you turns it over, this way and that, saying maybe it's this, maybe it's that. You think it through in front of everyone, talk it out, and share it. It's perfectly fine for it to have nothing to do with work. Naive things, childish things, likes and dislikes, strange things, we deliberately draw more and more of those elements in.

Painting in broad strokes, maybe the motivation behind all this is "I want to do interesting work." Ah, or perhaps "I want to become an interesting person" fits it better. And not just "interesting" in the comedy sense, mind you. If we made that a theme in the dojo, the conversation might just wander off toward something like, "Isn't there such a thing as boring but interesting?"

Yesterday was a Monday, one of our "dojo" days, and we even had AI join us. We had a good time. It's something we do every week, but for me, the "dojo" has truly become a "dojo" in the real sense.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today. "Sharing" is important beyond all reason. The place, and the sharing.
