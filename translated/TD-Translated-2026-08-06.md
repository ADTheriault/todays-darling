## Title: Shigesato Itoi will continue interviews for 100 days.
## Date: 2026-08-06
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

On a bit of a whim, I've started something new: interviewing you, the reader. (Confusing, isn't it?) Yesterday was the very first installment, and I asked, "What kind of child were you?" Remember?

The idea is to take a fresh look at how you think about your own childhood. And then to jot it down in your notebook, so you won't forget. Your answer might change later on, and even if it does, well, that just means you gave it some more thought, so that's perfectly fine too.

Your own childhood is the sort of thing you might never stop to consider unless someone asks. But then again, maybe you have thought about it, just a little. It lives in that hazy, half-formed territory, doesn't it?

Since there are surely things people would rather not share with others, I wrote, "Feel free to email me if you'd like." And then I received quite a number of emails. Many people said things like, "Thank you for this wonderful opportunity," which honestly put me at ease and made me happy.

And here's the thing: it fascinated me how many people said, "I was a bit of a sly child." We tend to imagine children as pure and innocent, yet so many people admitted, "I was a bit of a cunning kid." I'd count myself among them, for that matter. That kind of slyness is something I'd like to take another look at.

I'm prepared to keep this interview going all the way to a hundred questions. My hunch is that as you answer them one by one, a version of "yourself" you thought you knew but didn't quite will start to come into view. I'll weave together the simple questions and the wildly unexpected ones. No matter what, I'm going to see all hundred through.

So, on to the momentous second question.

No. 002: "Is there someone more important to you than yourself?"

Whatever your answer turns out to be, be sure to record it in your notebook. And of course, if you'd like, please write it down and send it to me by email.

Thank you for coming to Hobonichi today, too. Lately, all I ever think about is "Chiikawa" and "AI."
