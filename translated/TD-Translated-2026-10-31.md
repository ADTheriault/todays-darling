## Title: Find a question, and you can't help wanting to answer it
## Date: 2026-10-31
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

When some people spot a problem, they want to solve it. I suspect there are more of them than you'd think. Satoru Iwata, who was president of Nintendo, was one of them. Put something in front of him that was clearly a problem, and he'd immediately start working out a solution, and then go ahead and carry it out.

Even in conversation, if you asked him something, he'd try to answer it seriously. Even when it hadn't quite taken the form of a question, if he sensed there was a question hidden somewhere inside it, he'd go looking for the answer. People with that sort of instinct, he once told me, "are pretty common among engineers and programmers."

Come to think of it, whenever Iwata-san was coming over, I always made a point of piling all kinds of snacks on the table, right where he could see them. I'd told everyone at Hobonichi: "To Iwata-san, the snacks in front of him are a problem. He'll try to solve it, so he'll keep eating them." The result was always the same. The snacks would, without fail, disappear. For people like that, a problem is a snack. However hard it is, they can't resist wanting to work it out.

There's a reason this story came back to me. Lately, the resident Goddess of Disposal in our house has been weaving large patterns out of colorful vinyl tape. They're pieces for the Knitting Suzu Project, an earthquake recovery effort I've been doing together with Kodue Hibino. The round pieces everyone makes, something like little rugs, will decorate the walls of a dining hall in Suzu where the townspeople gather.

Until now, volunteers had been coming together at Hobonichi to make them, but we've also started accepting pieces sent in from all over the country. And our Goddess of Disposal has been knitting away at them with great enthusiasm. The announcement was hard to find, even on Hobonichi, so I asked her, "What made you decide to do it?" Her answer was simple: "They'd put out the pattern."

See the pattern (the question, the snack), and you knit it (answer it, eat it). Ah, so that's how it works, I thought, and found myself remembering Iwata-san.

Today's question, no. 088: "What kind of face do you have?" Have a think, and if you like, send us your answer by email.

Thank you for stopping by Hobonichi again today. If you're up for it, we'd love your help with the vinyl tape knitting.
