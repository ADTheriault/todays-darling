## Title: Karaoke, too, is a form of imitation.
## Date: 2026-08-29
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

When you do something that resembles "effort," people praise you. These days I've been doing this thing I call "Chiikawa copywork," and I'm fully prepared to be told it's an old man playing with cold water, that this isn't just learning at sixty but at seventy-seven. But because I plug away at it every single day, I get seen as "an old man making an effort," and somehow the praise starts creeping in. It's not like that at all. I'm just doing it, that's all. There are probably people who think I'm an idiot too, but I don't mind.

"Chiikawa copywork" is fun precisely because it's Chiikawa. And I deliberately call it "copywork," in the calligraphy sense, because there are discoveries to be made. It actually started when I watched the boss of my household doing calligraphy copywork and thought, "That looks kind of interesting." So the timing was just right.

I'd suspected for a long time that imitation is the foundation of learning. Once I actually began "Chiikawa copywork," that suspicion hardened into conviction. You come to feel, with real immediacy, just how bold and yet how meticulous the artist who drew the original was.

And there was still more to this thought!

Karaoke is copywork. You sing a song yourself because you think it's a good song. And when you try singing it with your own feelings, in your own body, you understand all over again how good the melody is, how good the lyrics are. Beyond that, you also come to appreciate the way the singer sings it, how wonderful that is. And you understand, all too clearly and unfortunately, just how far your own singing sits from the original that you're supposedly following. Sing karaoke and a respect for the song and the singer is born in you. See? Karaoke has exactly the same structure as copywork.

Ah, so it's copy-singing, then! I did too much of it today and hurt my throat.
