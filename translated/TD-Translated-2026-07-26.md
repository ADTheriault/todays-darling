## Title: To those of us on the verge of losing our sense of self
## Date: 2026-07-26
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

These days, I keep coming back to the idea of "subjectivity."

The other day, when I asked people about their favorite foods, it was because that's a question you can answer by letting your subjectivity run free.

In today's society, whenever we're asked about something, we tend to answer with a kind of "objective-sounding tidiness," saying things like "well, it could be this way, but then again it could be that way."

To put it half-jokingly, here's what I mean. "It keeps raining and I hate it" is subjectivity. But then, that same subjectivity, complaining about the rain, might land badly on someone from a region struggling with drought, where the reservoirs have run dry. That thought comes creeping in. And since I don't particularly want to voice my feelings about the rain badly enough to make others uncomfortable, I decide not to put out my subjective "I hate the rain."

And so I end up writing something with the subjectivity stripped away, like: "It keeps raining, and while some people wish it would stop, there are also people troubled by the lack of rain."

Repeat this enough times, and little by little, speaking without any subjectivity becomes a habit, and eventually you start living your life so as not to hold any subjectivity at all.

Even something like "I'm feeling great, my food tastes delicious" might make you hold back, thinking, "If someone who has no energy and no appetite hears this, it might hurt them, so maybe I shouldn't say it." Nobody wants to be hurt, and nobody wants to hurt others.

So we're all practicing, steadily, how to erase our subjectivity (and honestly, I'll admit it, I've become that way too).

But more than just saying that subjectivity matters, I've come to feel that subjectivity is the person, and that living is nothing more than the accumulation of subjectivity.

There's that famous opening passage from Natsume Soseki's <em>Kusamakura</em>: "Work by reason, and you grow edges. Push off with emotion, and you're swept away. Insist on your own will, and you feel confined. One way or another, the world of people is a hard place to live."

It's subjectivity that grows the edges, gets swept away, and feels confined. Subjectivity collides with things, gets lost, runs into trouble.

For now, maybe I'll just quietly jot my subjectivity down in a notebook.
