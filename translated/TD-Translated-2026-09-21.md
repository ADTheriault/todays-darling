## Title: Fried rice: my longing versus reality
## Date: 2026-09-21
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

At some point I developed an interest in fried rice, and started eating it all over the place. I used to think of it as something you had at the end of a Chinese meal, but lately I've been ordering it on its own at neighborhood Chinese joints. Just the fried rice, a little bowl of soup, maybe some pickles. I thought it might feel a bit lonely, that combination, but it doesn't. And fried rice, it seems to me, is rarely a disappointment.

Once you come to love fried rice, you start to feel a fondness for the cooks who make it so deftly. The famous flick of the wok, the fierce roar of gas flames beneath it, the eggs beaten and waiting, the meat, the scallions, all the ingredients prepped in advance, the clang and clatter of the iron ladle against the wok, the final shaping of the rice into a neat little dome, and then the shout to the server delivering the order: "Fried rice!" How could you not be filled with longing?

(omitted) So, slipping past the watchful eye of my household superior, the one who dreads the pointless accumulation of kitchen gadgets, I bought a wok by mail order. Not the iron one I'd been dreaming of, but titanium, and for the ladle I chose a bamboo spatula. Then a big bottle of rice oil. All of it different from what the pros use. The truth is, I arrived at these choices after careful consultation with AI.

I decided not to bother with cold rice either, going instead with rice that was still somewhat warm, and I resolved to resist the urge to flip the wok. I would stay an amateur and make honestly delicious fried rice. In moments like these, I am quite the earnest fellow.

One afternoon, with the kitchen chief away, I sprang into action. "Now's my chance." Wait, do I have the ingredients? Scallions, yes. There's pork too, but I'll go with bacon. Eggs, check. And some good rice in the freezer. Pepper, salt, a bit of kombu seasoning. This will work, I was sure of it.

By now I understood the concept completely. Fried rice is simply egg-over-rice, stir-fried. Add some aromatics and a few ingredients to that, and there's no way it could turn out bad. The titanium wok, the warmish rice, the bamboo spatula, all of it worked out fine. Next time it'll be ramen-style fried rice, with roast pork, bamboo shoots, and scallions. This Silver Week is turning out to be rather fun.
