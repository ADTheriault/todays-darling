## Title: Bride of the golden hairpin? I can't even read that title
## Date: 2026-11-15
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

It was Jun Miura who made the phrase "my boom" catch on. A boom, by definition, is something that sweeps up huge numbers of people, so a personal one, a "my" boom, ought to be a contradiction in terms. But these days everyone knows what it's like to have a tiny, private craze of their own, and so "my boom" became a boom in its own right. However narrow the obsession, however quickly it fizzles out, that's perfectly fine. I think part of the appeal was that you could treat the boom as disposable.

Nowadays the equivalent might be having an "oshi," someone you root for. But that word carries more emotional weight somehow, as though your whole heart is in it.

Thanks to Chiikawa, going to the movies became my boom for a while. If something looked promising, I'd book a ticket right away and just go. I had never been that light on my feet about it before. But after Godzilla -0.0, the trips to the theater stopped. The third season of Love Village started on Netflix, I got busy watching that, and from there my old boom of binge-watching dramas at home flared up all over again.

It started because Love Village 3 was so good. I could go on about it forever, but this third season is a masterpiece. It makes you feel, deep down, that every single person carries a history of their own, and thoughts and feelings that are theirs alone.

And now, for some reason, it's a Chinese drama series called Bride of the Golden Hairpin. It's a bit like the Indian films that were my boom not long ago: gorgeous, glamorous men and women driving the story forward at full throttle. To eyes grown used to a kind of next-door realism, it feels refreshing. Maybe it's the same feeling I had reading The Rose of Versailles, back in the day. Friday, Saturday, and Sunday were all consumed by Bride of the Golden Hairpin.

That said, I'm not exactly recommending it to everyone. Once you start, it gets its hooks in you and you keep watching episode after episode, but I haven't been telling the people around me, "You've got to see this." It's true that I'm watching because it's entertaining, though. It's complicated. I suppose that's about where my boom stands right now...
