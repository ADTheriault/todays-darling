## Title: The image of "serious damage"
## Date: 2026-09-22
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

In a sense, it's not all that unusual, but whenever "a large and powerful typhoon approaches the Japanese archipelago," the effects are enormous. Some people suffer direct damage, and no doubt a great many others are hit indirectly. I mean the people who were planning to hold outdoor events, those who work in transportation by air, land, or sea, and then, further removed, the people connected to all of that. The catering companies, for instance, must have had a hard time of it too. And of course, the harm reaches the spectators and passengers as well.

At times like these, the news reports things like "flights cancelled," "service suspended," "event called off." Sitting in your room watching television, it's easy to take in news of a "suspension" or a "cancellation" as someone else's problem, but for the people on the ground, at the actual site, these are grave decisions, and surely a very serious matter indeed. The scale of the damage and the impact is often described in numbers, too.

I don't think it's just my imagination, but reports of natural disasters seem to have grown far more frequent. This large, powerful Typhoon No. 25, too, has been covered almost without pause, and a great many people probably know exactly where it is right now.

And in the middle of all this, something occurred to me for the first time. Things like "cancellation," "flight cancelled," or "service suspended" are indeed a serious matter for the events and the transit systems, yet they are treated as something that "can happen," something "built into the system." No one wants to do it, but it is anticipated. Even when there's a huge loss involved, cancellation is a decision that can be made. It's what people call risk management. And it struck me that large-scale examples of it turn out to be surprisingly common.

In our daily lives, we go about in fear of this or that little failure, but our society today is actually making huge judgment calls all the time. During the pandemic, too, society changed so drastically, come to think of it.
