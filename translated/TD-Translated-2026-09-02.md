## Title: Oh, the passing of time, and how casually it slips away.
## Date: 2026-09-02
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

With a quiet, sinking sort of feeling, I think, "Ah, you young people," and then I call out to them. It's astonishing, I want to say, how time truly does slip away. To those of you in your thirties, to those around forty, to those who are fifty or so, I want to tell you this: the happy things, the painful things, the embarrassing things, they all go flying past behind you, like scenery through a train window. After all, the Miura Jun who was a student is now sixty-eight, the Kimura Takuya who was a boy is now a fifty-three-year-old father, and the fact that I'm the one saying all this at seventy-seven means none of it is really so strange.

You feel like you've just been eating your lunch on your break, and then it's already evening. That's the sort of pace at which the months and years pass by too, and eventually you come to feel this, half in astonishment and half as the most natural thing in the world.

Honestly, there's no real lesson in any of it. "Youth passes quickly, and learning is hard to come by," and so on, well, that's true enough. But it's simply a fact. There's no need to panic. Things turn out the way they turn out.

I've mentioned this a few times before, but when I was around fifty, I had my fortune read using the Four Pillars of Destiny. I was told that I had "a fate in which nothing you do will work out." At the time I was in a phase where I was hoping to find some encouragement, so being told the exact opposite got me down a little, I'll admit. And apparently the only way out of this "nothing will work out" fate was fully fifteen years away, starting at sixty-five. I remember thinking, by then I'll already be an old man, that's just cruel.

But, to rush ahead in the story a bit, before I knew it I had turned sixty-five, and in all the years leading up to it, life had been neither painful nor a failure. Nor, for that matter, did I feel that my luck had suddenly opened up at sixty-five. Time had simply passed, and before I knew it there I was, sixty-five. So that's how it is, I thought. Being alive is just time going by.

I found I was able to think, "Either way, that moment comes." There was no need to fret about time passing at all. What passes, passes. What grows old, grows old. What dies, dies. There's no "enlightenment" to it, because it's the same for everyone. And even after becoming an old man, life is, in its way, rather interesting.

So, today's question, no. 029: "Is this, perhaps, my flaw?" Shall we go looking for it? If it's there, it's there. If it isn't, it isn't. If you like, write it in an email and send it my way.
