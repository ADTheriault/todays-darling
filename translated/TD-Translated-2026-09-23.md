## Title: (Prior text omitted) Do the fools of this floating world get up and work?
## Date: 2026-09-23
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

There are countless things Shohei Ohtani has shown the world. Listing them one by one would go on forever, so I'll leave that aside. But there's one thing that seems to have slipped past most people's notice, and so, on behalf of Japan, the small island nation that produced him, I thought I'd offer my own modest word about it.

It's this: the man sleeps well.

He's said as much himself, and the people around him say it too. Apparently his routine is to sleep ten hours a day, naps included. Here is a person accomplishing all those extraordinary things, and he sleeps ten hours.

Somehow, that makes it easier for everyone to accept. If some perfectly ordinary person announced, "I sleep ten hours a day," they'd probably get a snide "Maybe you should work a little harder." I don't know if this is unique to Japan, but generally speaking, being awake and working has always been considered the "virtuous" thing.

And the people on the other end, the "work and work and work and work" types, seem to manage it by cutting into their sleep. The logic is that there's more you can do while you're awake. That's an easy enough idea to grasp. Squeezing in a bit more practice, sparing not a moment to prepare, to study. All of it is possible precisely because you're awake, your eyes open. Even playing around, even indulging in a hobby, you do it awake. The time spent sleeping was thought of as "nothing," a blank.

But by sleeping his fill, and by treating that sleep as "precious time" that feeds into his work, Shohei Ohtani has promoted "sleep (or rest)" to the status of important time. To put it in a way I don't much like, "sleep is a cost paid toward improved productivity," and if you manage it well, it yields results. Sleep has gone from being "empty time" to being "an essential nutrient."

Isn't that a rather wonderful thing? It's not as if Ohtani is the one who proposed this theory, but the fact that he's actually "sleeping well" and doing so much is a big deal. Still, imagine being someone who can even work hard at sleeping.

◆So the question, too, is simple. No. 050: "Are you sleeping well?"
