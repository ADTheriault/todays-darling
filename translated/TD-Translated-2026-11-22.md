## Title: Come to think of it, it goes back to when I was a kid: on paper airplanes
## Date: 2026-11-22
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Sometimes a kid who plays Little League keeps at it through high school and eventually becomes a pro ballplayer. When I stop and think about my own life, I find myself wondering whether what I do now is really the same thing I've been doing ever since elementary school.

There's a memory about a paper airplane that I've never forgotten. I was in fifth grade or so. One day I folded a sheet of memo paper into a plane, and it came out beautifully. It flew astonishingly well. I'd folded it the way I always did, so it was pure luck, but it glided through the air for so long, so smoothly, that I just stood there admiring it.

My classmates were impressed too, and soon everyone was grabbing whatever paper was lying around and folding planes of their own. But theirs didn't exactly sail along the way mine had. It seemed the palm-sized memo pad I had, the kind where you tear off one sheet at a time, was just the right paper for the job. I handed out sheets to my friends one by one, but we were all playing so eagerly that before long we ran out.

So I went to the little shop at school that sold stationery and bought a whole new pad. Here, everybody, use this! Now we can make as many paper airplanes as we want. (No, no, I wasn't some rich kid or anything.) We could hold contests to see whose plane flew the farthest. The craze didn't last all that long, but for a while we were all completely absorbed in that game.

I wanted to play some new kind of game, so I tried to build the "playground" for it, the system, you might say. Handing out the equipment, that sort of thing. I've been doing that all along. Maybe what I was doing with those paper airplanes was my version of Little League.

Who'd have thought that at my age I'd be looking back on what I've done with my life like this... huh.
