## Title: So afraid of empty time that I got rid of it, and then...
## Date: 2026-11-19
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I feel a great many things, think a great many things, turn a great many things over in my mind. And I also feel that I wish I had more empty time. Which gets me thinking about empty time. Which in turn gets me wondering what I ought to do with empty time. See? There I go again, thinking. Filling up the precious empty time I had. This is a kind of illness, probably. It's my illness, and the illness of everyone living in this day and age. We can't bear empty time.

Back when I was in elementary school or so, I had empty time. With nothing else for it, I'd often just lie there staring at the ceiling. Not television, not manga or books, just the ceiling, or sometimes the chest of drawers, or the sliding paper doors. "Why do they call a chest of drawers a chest of drawers?" I'd wonder, again and again, and then give it up. But that question, "Why do they call a chest of drawers a chest of drawers?", may well be where all my thinking began.

Empty time made me restless, and its blankness was hard to bear. How could I fill it? How could I make it go away? That's all I ever thought about, and I schemed, experimented, and put plan after plan into action. For decades upon decades, that's all I've been doing.

There are passive ways to erase empty time, too. You can just watch TV or read manga. But when those turn out to be dull, they start to feel like empty time themselves, which makes them dangerous.

So, to fill my empty time myself, in my own way, I'm forever stocking up on raw material, nibbling at it, swallowing it down. What lies before me becomes a mountain of raw material for filling empty time. The books I don't read, the recordings I never watch, the work I've left half done, the food I want to eat, the scenery I want to gaze at, the places I want to visit, the pleasures other people are enjoying: all of it will someday fill my empty time, and so it keeps piling up until the warehouse is full. Having my empty time filled becomes simply the way things are. And once you've done away with all that frightening empty time, you find it hard to breathe.

If any of this strikes a chord, please write in and tell me. Thank you, as always, for stopping by Hobonichi today. I didn't set out to write anything gloomy. I just wrote it, that's all.
