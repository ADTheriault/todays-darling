## Title: Guided by the trailer, I watched "We Are Aliens."
## Date: 2026-09-26
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

When you go to see a single movie like <em>Chiikawa</em> six times, you end up watching the trailers in the theater exactly that many times. And naturally, among those trailers, you find something that catches your eye. Which is how I came to think, I want to see this, and headed out on opening day for an animated film called <em>We Are Aliens</em>.

Why did I want to see it? The reason was the images on the screen. No, really, I'd taken in almost no information beyond that. It was just that those visuals, the way the backgrounds and objects and characters were rendered, struck me as tremendously fresh and new.

It wasn't only realism. There was a hand-drawn touch to it too. You could call it painterly, or photographic, but it also brought to mind the manga of Taiyo Matsumoto and the like. This was not a foreign work. It was unmistakably Japan, somewhere around the Heisei era. If I lingered on the images, I found I only wanted to keep looking at them.

So I decided not to look at anything else, not the story, not the introductions or the explanations. First and foremost, it was the pictures, and the way those pictures moved looked interesting. And if the film itself turned out to be a dud, well, so be it. I made my theater reservation in something like the spirit of drawing a lottery ticket.

The title, <em>We Are Aliens</em>, has a vaguely science-fiction flavor to it, and it also feels almost like a parody. Having watched it, I can say this title suited the film well, though I also think it would have been plenty interesting under a different one.

I won't say too much about the content, but what it brought to my mind was <em>Stand by Me</em>. That said, there's none of the dry, sun-baked feel of a Lucas film. From beginning to end, whether rain or sweat or blood or river or tap water or fish tank, water is being drawn on the screen the entire time. It was a film soaked through for a solid two hours, and that in itself was remarkable.

And what it tells, in a sense, is a self-introduction. It's the self-introduction of a person called "we," spoken by the two protagonists. There will surely be viewers in the audience who sync up with it, thinking, that's me too. And that, after all, is probably exactly what the person introducing themselves was hoping for. I find myself wanting to learn more about the planning, the screenplay, the director.
