## Title: What we have outside the walls that isn't found within
## Date: 2026-11-11
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Yesterday I wrote that the 78 years I've lived really have been long. Not short in the least. This isn't exactly a continuation of that, but I've been thinking a little more about the length of time.

Please don't take this as anything grim. Say someone commits a crime, is tried, and goes to prison. Three years, five years, twenty years, life. That stretch of time is what we call a "sentence." During it, they should have the basics: meals, sleep, hygiene, safety. In a sense, you could say their right to live hasn't been taken from them any more than it has from the people on the outside. So what is it they don't have? Countless "freedoms."

When to eat, and what to eat. What time to go to bed and what time to get up, where to go when you step out of the house, whom to see, what to do, whether to grit your teeth through a job you hate or just skip it. Tucked inside all the things we on "the outside" do as a matter of course, without finding them the least bit remarkable, is a great heap of a precious commodity called "freedom." Simply taking most of it away is enough to make a punishment.

If someone who has done something terrible is handed, say, a sentence of five years, it means five years' worth of "freedom" is taken from them. Losing "freedom" for five years is considered heavy enough to balance out the wrong they did. And society agrees that being deprived of "freedom" for that span of time is a very great punishment indeed. This may sound like I'm being overly logical about it, but that's how enormous the value of "freedom" is!

So "freedom" is something human beings simply can't do without, I find myself thinking, rather late in the day, and getting a little sentimental about it. Well, gosh, I suppose I may be squandering my freedom a bit...

Today's question, no. 099: "When do you feel most free?"
