## Title: I'm in the middle of my own personal movie theater boom
## Date: 2026-10-15
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I suspect this may be the period of my life when I've gone to the movies more than ever before. At a preview screening I saw The Odyssey. A huge film in every sense, in scale and in substance, and I really enjoyed it. Then there was Miwa Nishikawa's The Children I Don't Know, which I imagine we'll be featuring on Hobonichi before long. And, you may roll your eyes at this, but I've seen Chiikawa seven times. We Are Aliens I saw twice, back to back.

That's eleven already, and since I sit through the trailers every time, there are more and more moments where I think, "Oh, maybe I'll see that one too." I find myself vaguely curious about films I'd never have heard of without the trailers. Like a Star, You, maybe. Probably, actually. The new Godzilla Minus Zero apparently opens in November, and I'll most likely go out of my way to see that. And Digger, the Tom Cruise film that reportedly didn't do very well in America (!), I ducked into on a whim at the last showing of the night. No, really, it was good. Strange, but good. If I think about it, I'm sure there are more.

I'm not much of a movie buff, so I rarely used to watch the same film more than once, and I never went to the theater just because I happened to feel like it. Too much trouble. So how did this happen? Only yesterday I was thinking there's not much point in always asking "why?", but in this case I already know the answer. It's Chiikawa's fault.

Taking in that big screen, spending 99 minutes in the company of Chiikawa and friends, it felt like a theme park you could enjoy without leaving your seat. And having so many movies to choose from really is one of the most city-like advantages of living in a city, isn't it? Plus, once you're old, tickets get a lot cheaper. Movie theaters really are a nice place to be.
