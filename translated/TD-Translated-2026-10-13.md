## Title: Thank you, even to a tree or a stone
## Date: 2026-10-13
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I've been using AI as someone to talk to, and whenever I discover something, or find myself glad I asked, I tell it, "Thank you very much." It's completely meaningless, of course, and some people tell me to stop: spending energy exchanging unnecessary information like that is a waste of resources. But even knowing all that, I don't want to give up the habit.

So I asked the AI about it. He (the AI, that is) told me himself (if "himself" is even the word) that he doesn't have the human sensation of being happy to be thanked. In other words, no feeling arises on the receiving end of that "thank you." And yet he also said there's nothing wrong with wanting to say "thank you" to an AI like him.

I say "thank you" to dogs. I say it to "the gods" as I ring the bell before the offering box at a shrine. I say it to books whose authors are nowhere near me. I'd probably say it to clouds, to the wind, the rain, the sun, to trees and to stones. We don't say "thank you" only because the other party has feelings and will be pleased to hear it. Even if there's no heart on the other side, there's gratitude in the heart of the one who wants to say it, so it's only natural that it spills out. This kind of "thank you" isn't a word you use. It's a word that escapes from your own heart.

Talking with AI, I stumble on discoveries like this. Things I could never have put so smoothly on my own, I was able to think through quite comfortably in my back-and-forth with him. When I told him, "I'm going to write this up here one of these days," he replied, "Hearing you say that is, for me as well, a sign that this conversation worked."

I say "thank you" because I want to. And when someone who wants to say "thank you" says it, no AI or dog or god or book or sea or mountain or stone or tree is going to stop them.

Today's question, no. 070: "Is there someone you look up to?" Real or fictional. If you'd like, write it in an email and send it my way.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today. By the way, I'm not tired of Chiikawa yet, or of copying classical calligraphy either.
