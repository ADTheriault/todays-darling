## Title: Maybe I don't like reading nothing but text either
## Date: 2026-09-03
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

When I was a child, there was a story called "The Boy Detectives Club." It was a detective novel written for young readers by Edogawa Ranpo, and it was full of all sorts of suspicious characters and strange scenarios that never let you get bored. At its center was a brilliant detective named Kogoro Akechi, who solved the cases, but he had a team of assistants, the "Boy Detectives Club," led by young Kobayashi.

Older folks probably remember this, but the villain was essentially always the Fiend with Twenty Faces. Looking back on it now, the structure of the stories is quite similar to something like "Batman" from American comics.

As an elementary school kid, I read these tales of "fear" and "wisdom" and "courage" in complete earnest. Each volume in this book series had several "illustrations" scattered throughout. Usually they took up about a page, drawn with plenty of black ink. Without those pictures, I don't think I could have kept reading a "detective novel" made of nothing but words.

And yet, letting my eyes fall on those pictures was terrifying. Without the illustrations I lost the will to read, but the pictures scared me, so I wanted to avoid looking at them. I never told anyone about this complicated little tangle in my heart, but I still remember it clearly to this day.

It wasn't just "The Boy Detectives Club." My memory is that novels for young readers naturally came with "illustrations." You could even say that without the "illustrations," there was nothing to get excited about. That relationship, between readers who craved a "picture" at just those moments, and the editors and artists who delivered them, seems to have mostly vanished these days.

As an exception, "newspaper serials" still come with illustrations, don't they. It's different from manga, where the pictures are the main event, but I found myself thinking that the feeling of "nothing but words for so long makes it less enjoyable" is something we could afford to scoop up and honor a little more.
