## Title: Beyond the top of the vocal range. The darkness seen with closed eyes.
## Date: 2026-08-05
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

So, do you do karaoke, that sort of thing? Even if not karaoke, do you ever sing? When you sing, there's this thing called vocal range, and at some point, both on the low end and the high end, the notes just stop coming out. There are notes other people can hit that you simply can't. But what if you don't actually voice them, can you imagine higher and higher notes without limit? Just imagining seems like it should be possible, doesn't it? Shall we give it a try? This is something I thought up and tested back in my twenties. What I found was that as I went up an octave, and then another, at some point I was just cycling through the same range over and over without realizing it (I wrote about this on Hobonichi back in 1998, too). It turns out there's a limit even to the "imagination" of sound. When I first wrote about this, Yosuke Yamashita, the jazz musician, told me, "That was really interesting," and I remember being tremendously happy about it.

Now then, I recently discovered something along these same lines again. At night, in that stretch between getting into bed and drifting off to sleep, it's no good to let your mind wander to pointless things, so I decided that after closing my eyes I would picture some lovely scene and just gaze at it there behind my eyelids. But this "lovely scene" I wanted to gaze at turned out to be surprisingly hard to conjure up. After trying all sorts of things, lately I've settled on this baffling theme of "the dark reaches of outer space," and I've made that my thing to look at. Since it's basically just "darkness," it's fine for it to be vague. If I stare at it and things go well, I sink pleasantly into sleep.

And while doing this, at one point I realized something: I'm looking at this "darkness" with my dominant eye. I'm viewing the "darkness" mainly with my left eye, so what is my right eye doing? It's providing "support," you see, backing up the dominant eye. Now, I can look at the "darkness" with just my right eye too, but somehow that leaves me unsettled. And that's it. Today's story comes to its abrupt little end right here.
