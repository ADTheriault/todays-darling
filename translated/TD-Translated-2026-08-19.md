## Title: What Chiikawa taught me.
## Date: 2026-08-19
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

These days I think about nothing but "AI" and "Chiikawa." Well, what can you do. They're worth thinking about, and what I think about is mine to decide, so I'll keep on thinking about "AI" and "Chiikawa." This too is what it means to value the "subjective."

When my daughter's daughter said, "I want to watch Chiikawa one more time," I asked her, "Shall we go watch it together with Shige-chan?" She fell silent, her face gone blank, as if all the warmth had drained out of it. Later she told me, in words I'd never heard from her before, "I didn't know what I was supposed to say." Ah, so it was something you'd never experienced or even thought about. I see.

Under the influence of Chiikawa (that is, of its creator, Nagano-san), I microwaved some frozen rice and ate it with butter and soy sauce. I've long known how good this is, but somehow it always got looked at as "the kind of thing a weirdo does," so ever since I entered the working world, I'd held myself back quite a bit. Natto is allowed, eggs are allowed, but butter is off-limits? I felt that way, and yet I never did it.

But thanks to my own personal "Chiikawa movement," I did it, and with a clear conscience too. It's delicious, of course. In the same spirit, I ordered some Myojo Charumera noodles. Both of these are things that, within the value system of adulthood, become the choice you make while thinking, "Well, this'll do."

But Nagano-sensei, and the Chiikawa characters too, truly eat these things saying, "This is delicious." The way it's depicted is so natural, so contented, so cool. Stronger than the mock-critic games of some gourmet blowhard, all lecture and "that stuff's no good," is a simple "This is delicious."

I think I can go back to being someone who eats butter-soy-sauce rice and Charumera with genuine relish. I won't get all preachy and quote proverbs like "I learn only to be content with what I have." No, it's just "This is delicious."
