## Title: Just back from a trip and writing in good spirits.
## Date: 2026-09-13
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I went to Kyoto for the first time in a while. My schedule happened to have a little room in it, so I managed to drop by two of the places I know well. Of course, part of it is wanting to eat this dish or that dish, but it's also about stopping in, sitting down to a meal, exchanging a few words (not always just a few) with the people who run the place, and confirming that the door at the entrance, the counter, the little wooden menu tags are all just as they used to be, remembering, ah, yes, it was always like this. It's that whole experience I go there for. And when I show my face and they're glad to see me, well, that alone fills me with an absurd amount of happiness.

Let me think, in Kyoto there turned out to be several places I want to visit in that way. One of them was an okonomiyaki restaurant, but it's closed down now, so I can't go anymore. Maybe I could count the croquette shop the butcher runs, too. As I expected, they're all places from the period when I was going to Kyoto frequently. Come to think of it, the only towns with places like that for me are Kyoto and Kesennuma. It's a bit of a shame I can't think of anywhere like that in Maebashi, but, ah, there's the Harashimaya for grilled manju, isn't there.

The real reason for the trip, I suppose it was a project organized by one of Kyoto's universities, was my job taking part in a "three-way talk" up at that spot at Kiyomizu Temple. That was thoroughly enjoyable in its own right, but as a kind of bonus I got to stop by those familiar shops, and I was also lucky that the "Ryokan" exhibition at the Hosomi Museum happened to open this very Saturday, so I could go see it.

I love it, you know, Ryokan's calligraphy. Not "it's good" or anything like that. "I love it" is what fits best. Following the way the characters on display are written with my eyes, imagining it, something wells up in me. It surprised me, too, that Kitaoji Rosanjin, who always struck me as such a sharp-tongued man, praised Ryokan's writing to the skies.

And so, back in Tokyo, the first thing I did was some "Chiikawa calligraphy practice."
