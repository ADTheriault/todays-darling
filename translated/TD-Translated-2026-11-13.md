## Title: Everything changes, that much is a given, but still
## Date: 2026-11-13
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Did the people who built the pyramids ever say to one another, "I wonder what people in the future will think when they see this"? Did they imagine, even a little, how they'd be seen? They were the ones actually building the thing, after all, so I doubt it ever crossed their minds that someone would one day ask, "How on earth did they make it?"

When I was in elementary school, a prank caught on for a while among the dumber kids: drawing fake chest hair on yourself. Back then, men like the baseball star Shigeo Nagashima, the sumo wrestler Asashio, and the leading men in foreign films all showed off their chest hair, and it looked cool. Who could have guessed that the men of later generations would get rid of so much of their body hair! Neither the kids nor the grown-ups of that time could have imagined it, not by a single hair.

The Imperial Palace in today's Chiyoda Ward stands on the site of the old Edo Castle. The people who saw that castle as part of their everyday scenery could never have imagined it would become the Imperial Palace and look the way it does now.

When you're living in a given era, you can't really understand the eras that came before it or the ones still to come. You can forecast, predict, imagine, even fantasize, if you try. But the changes almost always turn out to be on a completely different scale. Then again, we don't even understand the era we're living in...

Someday, a teacher in the far-off future might tell their students, "So up until this period, there was an institution called marriage, and people lived in units called families." Then again, teachers and students might not exist anymore either. And the language people speak will probably have changed completely.

Things change, you know, so much that it's beyond imagining. And yet, even amid all that, some things stay the same. I have a feeling that if you live with your eyes fixed on the things that don't change, the past, the present, and the future all become a little easier to see.
