## Title: For the first time, I saw dead leaves
## Date: 2026-11-27
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

"Dead leaves scattering in the dusk," "the dead leaves of autumn," they turn up in songs all the time, don't they, dead leaves. There's even a song named for them outright, the one the French call "The Dead Leaves" and we know as "Autumn Leaves." I, too, would catch a little of that image from the songs and, in a pleasantly tipsy sort of way, think, "...ah, yes."

But dead leaves are real things, lying around here and there. And when I saw them, or tramped over them, I'm not sure I ever felt much of anything. Lots of leaves on the ground, I'd think, or, they've changed color, yellow, red. Careful, wet leaves after the rain will send you sliding. I'd go out of my way to admire the trees in their autumn colors, yet once the leaves had fallen, I paid them no mind at all. At most, I might have spared a thought of thanks for whoever was sweeping them up.

But this year, believe it or not, for the very first time in my life, I was walking down the street, looked at the dead leaves lying there, and was moved, quietly and deeply. Alas (yes, "alas," I chose that grand old word on purpose), these are dead leaves. I see, trees. I see, leaves. So you've fallen, have you, fluttering down.

I can't say yet what my heart is actually thinking. Only that, for the first time in my life, I thought: so these are dead leaves... I suppose some of you will ask what on earth I'm going on about. The fallen leaves, the withered leaves I must have seen year after year, didn't just pass before my eyes this time. It felt as though my heart saw them. Maybe this is me discovering my own aging. What once was living falls. It takes on color, but sooner or later it will vanish. People tread on fallen leaves without feeling the least bit sorry for them, walk on, and forget, and every so often they put them in a song.

Wow. So this is why people sing about dead leaves? The poets, East and West, who wrote about dead leaves, did they have this feeling while they were still young? Precocious, the lot of them. Me, I'm only now getting around to "dead leaves, huh." Quite the late bloomer. But you and I both, we felt the dead leaves, didn't we.

I wonder, those of you who've stuck with me this far, do you feel it too, somewhere in your heart: "dead leaves..."? Anyway, whatever comes to mind, please, send me a note.
