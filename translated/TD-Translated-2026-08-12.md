## Title: Looking back on 10 years, how was it?
## Date: 2026-08-12
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

People used to say, "Ten years makes an age." I think it was meant to convey something like, "Once ten years have passed, it's already the distant past." That if you're still carrying the same feelings you had ten years ago, well, you're already behind the times.

And I often found there was a lot of truth in it. Setting childhood aside, ten years as an adult feels short. I'll find myself recalling something from five or six years ago as though it were just the other day. And yet, once ten years have gone by, all sorts of things have changed. That, I think, was the pace at which the times moved. It's only a vague sense, but we used to mark off the years in tens, dividing up the eras that way.

But in the IT world, ten years was far more than "an age." It moved at a speed where the word "dog years" felt entirely natural. Yes, and just as a dog of ten is well into its "senior" years, perhaps the information society is already a "senior" itself, grown old.

And when it comes to everything outside the world of information, ten years ago, twenty years ago, thirty years ago, it all looks like more or less the same era carrying on. Whether or not you want to call that "stagnation," I really don't mind either way. The bullet trains got a little faster, the department stores gave way to sprawling electronics chains, people stopped carrying cash around, and yet nothing much really changed. And as things stand, there's no one out there to speak of a ten-years-from-now that young people might actually feel hopeful about.

Once you've followed all this and thought, "Yes, that's exactly how it is," the question becomes what each of us, each of our own "we," actually wants to do. What we "like." What we think is "good." Asking ourselves, and each other, all over again, that's what feels so terribly important to me right now.
