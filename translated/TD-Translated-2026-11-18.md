## Title: Don't try to beat phone scammers at their own game
## Date: 2026-11-18
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Every so often you hear someone telling, with a certain pride, how they got one of those scam calls and "played along, pretending to be fooled, and ended up fooling the scammer instead." When I was younger, I probably would have found that hilarious, and I might even have thought I'd like to try it myself. But the person I am now would say, "You really shouldn't do that sort of thing."

People are naturally drawn to whatever is dangerous, shady, uncanny, or out of the ordinary. Perhaps that's an ability we developed for sensing danger. Or perhaps it's a spirit of adventure, the urge to blaze new trails. There's even the old saying that you can't catch the tiger's cub without going into the tiger's den. But a little knowledge is a dangerous thing. And plenty of people who go out for wool come home shorn.

It's no good, you see. At some point, how should I put it, you start taking on the same values, picking up the same rules. It happens all the time in dramas about undercover cops or spies: you breathe the same air and live the same life, and eventually you catch yourself asking, "Wait, what was the difference again?" Something you started as a joke turns serious. Look around carefully and you'll find it everywhere. I've had a few whoa-there moments of my own. Curiosity, a taste for fun, a love of adventure: these things are always tempting you to stick your nose into all sorts of places.

But I think you have to draw a line somewhere. Turning the tables on a phone scammer means you've stepped into their ring, doesn't it? You're playing their game. That's exactly why you mustn't do it.

These days, scam websites and fake articles have gotten incredibly sophisticated. Even when you come across them, you shouldn't go "pretending to be fooled" or trying to "beat" the bad guys. Stay just as you always are, and keep within your own bounds: if there's something you can actually do, do it, and leave it at that.

I know this sounds like an old man's lecture, but it matters, so I'm saying it anyway.
