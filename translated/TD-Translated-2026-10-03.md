## Title: The festival's over, and I'm not even sad.
## Date: 2026-10-03
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

It won't make the news anywhere, being the kind of purely local affair that it is, but yesterday, at the "Kanda Nishikicho Ennichi" festival, our team, the "Hobonichi Hipparees," won the "Tug-of-War Tournament 2026," reclaiming the trophy we'd once crafted ourselves out of prize money from years past. Who knew a victory after three long years could feel this good? The Hobonichi crowd was positively choked up with tears of joy.

Truly, it's a bit of local news, a shameless bit of self-promotion, the result of a competition that may well strike some as trivial. But for the neighbors of Kanda Nishikicho in Chiyoda Ward, it was a "festival" that had everyone fired up for the first time in ages.

Now, Kanda has always had the "Kanda Matsuri," a grand festival known all across the country. There's no payment for the labor, of course, and come to think of it, no trophy to fight over either, and the preparations that come around once every two years are an enormous, exhausting undertaking. And yet, "We do it." Because this is Kanda.

It all started, really, back when our company was first set to move to this neighborhood. The elders of the local association pressed us firmly on one point: "You'll be taking part in the festival, won't you?" W-well, of course we'll take part! It's an absolute blast, after all.

But, aside from the "Kanda Matsuri," there also turned out to be this thing called the "Kanda Nishikicho Ennichi," and that one caught us completely off guard. Still, "We do it!" Because that's just how Nishikicho is.

Four years ago, we didn't just set up our signature "Twice-the-Fun Yo-Yo Fishing" stall, so very Hobonichi. We also threw ourselves into the "Tug-of-War Tournament" with a cry of, "Let's take this seriously!" And so, Hobonichi, a company that usually acts like a bunch of goof-offs who skip club activities to head straight home, gritted its teeth and actually went and won the whole thing. Which got everyone in town going, "Oh yeah? Then so will we!" They caught fire too. These things are always more fun the more seriously you take them.

And so, this year, 2026, the number of participating teams swelled to 48. The venue moved to a larger spot, and by the neighborhood association chairman's eyeball estimate, the crowd of spectators and participants, young and old, men and women alike, came to "around 5,000." A roaring success. A festival that brings neither profit nor loss, offering, for this one night only, the thrill of winning and losing. Honestly, "having a neighborhood" really is a wonderful thing, isn't it?
