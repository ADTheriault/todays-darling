## Title: If only we could look forward to the future again...
## Date: 2026-10-20
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

On July 20, 1969, fifty-seven years ago now, human beings set foot on the moon and planted an American flag there. That mission made people all over the world feel, all at once, that this was the kind of future that had been waiting for us. And people kept going to the moon after that. By the time of Apollo 17, twelve astronauts had walked on its surface. I have to admit I'd completely forgotten that part.

We talk about "the future" all the time, but in the manga of the old days, a "telephone like a wristwatch" was one of its great symbols. So was the giant television screen hanging on the wall. Robots that cleaned the house or carried out the dinner, that was the future too. So were cars that drove themselves with no one behind the wheel. Yet the futures people dreamed up back then have, by now, pretty much all come true.

Up until a certain point, in those fondly remembered times, when we imagined manga-like things and waited eagerly for them to become real, there was, quite literally, something to look forward to.

These days, I can't seem to find anything like that. Stories set in "the near future" show bleak ruins, or portray humanity's despair and division, but that's hardly a future anyone is looking forward to.

I suspect there are people out there wondering where we might find a future that can be spoken of in the same breath as anticipation. Just as someone once set out to make humans walk on the moon, some people talk about undersea cities or settling on Mars, but even those feel rather far removed from the kind of thing you look forward to.

A future worth looking forward to is hard to put into pictures or diagrams, but every so often, in a hazy sort of way, even someone like me finds himself thinking there must be at least a little room to imagine one. For me, it would be a future in which human beings can be close to other human beings.
