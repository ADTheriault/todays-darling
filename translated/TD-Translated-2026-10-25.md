## Title: Swimming right above the Mariana Trench
## Date: 2026-10-25
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Don't you think the resolve you need is different when you're swimming in a shallow pool where little kids are paddling around, compared with swimming in the ocean after someone tells you, "The water here is ten meters deep"? But really, doing the breaststroke over the Mariana Trench (depth: 10,924 meters) and doing the crawl in a pool one meter deep amount to exactly the same thing. However deep or shallow it is to the bottom, you're just floating up near the surface and swimming either way.

Still, if someone tried to scare you by saying, "The bottom of the sea you're in is ten thousand meters down," wouldn't that frighten you a little? It struck me that this fear of depth might be a lot like the way people relate to one another.

You know how, when people meet a president, or His Majesty, or a billionaire, or a huge star, or some great scholar, they describe a kind of almost-fearful feeling, as if this person were somehow different from the neighbors they see every day? They say the person "has an aura," or is "larger than life." I think that's the same thing as the depth of the sea.

Whether it's Bob Dylan or Einstein, the one standing there is a human being of ordinary human height, isn't it? And you're a human being too. Just as swimming is the same no matter how deep the sea is, no matter how great a master or how famous a name, their size and their looks and the things they do don't change. To buy a 200-yen drink, they pay 200 yen. Sitting down in a chair, walking a little way, it's the same for everyone.

If you only ever swim at the surface, the sea floor doesn't matter. In the ordinary business of daily life, a person's position or stature has nothing to do with anything. Of course, people need to show each other respect. But that much respect is something you owe the kids down the street, too.

Well, I started out imagining swimming over the Mariana Trench and the whole thing rolled off in a direction I never expected, but it's kind of interesting, don't you think?
