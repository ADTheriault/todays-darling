## Title: What is willpower, anyway?
## Date: 2026-09-16
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

"In the end, it all comes down to mental strength," you often hear. It's the sort of thing people say about sports, mostly. But what mental strength actually means, I've never really understood. They tell you to stay focused, to keep the tension up, and yet sometimes tension is exactly what makes you stiff, what keeps you from performing at your best. So then, should you just relax? Well, if you're too relaxed, maybe you lose that burst of explosive power. I don't know. For someone like me, it's a mystery.

Generally speaking, in the world of top-level players, where abilities are more or less evenly matched, a tiny difference is what produces a big gap in the results. But in most competitions, the field is a mix of people with genuinely different levels of skill. It might come down to mental strength, sure, but often there's already a real gap in ability before you even get to that.

I've spoken with people who've made their careers as professionals in the sports world, heard all sorts of stories, and when these pro athletes talk about the truly legendary figures among them, they often put it this way: "I completely lost every ounce of confidence I'd had." One of them told me, "The gap in ability between the very best players is wider than the gap between an amateur and a pro." How terrifying is that, really.

Right, mental strength. That's what I was talking about. The idea that sheer willpower can carry you through is an old one. And not just old, I suspect it's actually wrong. But here's the thing about this word, or rather this concept, of mental strength: it's almost always the fans, the supporters, the people who aren't actually out there doing it, who bring it up. If the person actually in the fight says "this will come down to mental strength," then it becomes something more actionable, something closer to science, than mere "mental strength."

People who aren't the ones competing shouldn't be so quick to insist that someone "lacks mental strength." That's what I've come to believe.
