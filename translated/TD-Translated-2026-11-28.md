## Title: All the games we used to play for free
## Date: 2026-11-28
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

This was more than half a century ago, but there's a story I still tell now and then, almost as if I were bragging.

I wasn't even twenty yet. I had dropped out of school and was nothing more than an idle fool. Still, I had a friend, and since his university had been barricaded shut, he had nothing to do either. He was no fool, but he was just as idle. I can't recall everything we got up to, but we met often and killed a lot of time together. As far as I remember, everything we did was free.

One of those free amusements was hypnosis. Back when we were in junior high, a book called "An Introduction to Hypnosis" had come out, and if you read it and gave it a go, it turned out you really could hypnotize people. My friend and I had both read it a few years earlier. Somehow we got it into our heads to try it.

I took the role of the hypnotist, and my friend was more than willing to be the subject. Our curiosities lined up so perfectly that we were able to run all sorts of experiments that later made for good stories. Oh, we tried so many different suggestions. We'd hash it out like a comedy duo writing a routine, let's try this, let's try that, settle on a theme, and then I'd put him under.

Among those games was a suggestion to make one particular number disappear. "When you come out of hypnosis, the number seven will have vanished from the world completely." This is what's called a posthypnotic suggestion, one that stays with you even after you've woken up.

If I gave him an arithmetic problem whose answer was seven, he'd say, "I don't know." If I showed him a clock face and had him read off the hours in order, it went: "One o'clock, two, three, four, five, six, eight, nine..." When he recited his multiplication tables, he jumped straight from the sixes to the eights. When he counted on his fingers, both hands together came to nine.

I'm not sure it's wise to play around with this sort of thing too much, but once I had lifted the posthypnotic suggestion as well, my friend and I were genuinely moved. "So that's what numbers are. That's what counting really is." Even now, I still believe that's simply how the world works.
