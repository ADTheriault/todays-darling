## Title: Thinking about things that don't become new.
## Date: 2026-08-22
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

People love to say things like, "This is how the times are now." The media says it, and so do the people who fancy themselves ahead of the curve. As for me, I'm apparently seen as someone fond of novelty, and I sometimes get asked for my opinions on such things.

But it's nothing to brag about, and the truth is, I'm not the least bit new. I'm not one of those people who clings to old ways and calls it "having standards," but honestly, I keep a pretty careful distance from anything new.

The same goes for how I work. I'm not doing anything new there either. Every time some theory becomes the topic of the day, some "such-and-such organization" or "so-and-so management style," I get asked in interviews, "Hobonichi is one of those such-and-such organizations, isn't it?" And I'm left at a loss, because I've honestly never really thought about it. I might scramble to read a book or two on the subject after the fact, but since I never built my work around any theory to begin with, even when something strikes me as insightful, that's all it amounts to.

Whenever there's a theory or a principle, and the idea is to work toward it, I can't help feeling it tends to lock you into a mold. Having a clear mold is convenient for streamlining things, for smoothing everything out, but getting drawn into that kind of competition just isn't our strength.

Still, whether things are new or old, whatever the era may be, I think there are things that stay the same no matter what. Like, people way back in ancient times wouldn't have liked being treated this way either. Or, if someone did this for you, wouldn't that make you happy. Or, anyone would fall for a thing like this.

These are the "feelings of the heart" that exist within me and within everyone else. Throughout the long history of humankind, they've never changed, and in the times ahead, they'll seem to change and yet stay the same.

As for what exactly those unchanging things are, I do think we should keep considering it seriously, sparing no effort. The people of old spoke of "learning the new by studying the old," and it's no different today.
