## Title: Things like "I like it" or "I love it."
## Date: 2026-08-10
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

When humans set out to make machines do something, the direction of progress has always been the same. Faster, stronger, more efficient, more abundant. This is easy to grasp, and easy to express in numbers. Look how much faster it's become, how much stronger, how much more it can do. "Fast, strong, efficient, abundant" can be compared, in a sense, by anyone, using numbers and the like.

And this kept developing, reaching a level the human eye can no longer see, no longer keep up with. What's more, this kind of evolution wasn't limited to the physical. It extended into the realm of what the brain does, too. From the era when we called them electronic calculators all the way to today's "generative AI," there has been an evolution almost violent in its intensity.

But that progress is centered on what can be compared through numbers and symbols, on what anyone can clearly evaluate. If "fast, strong, efficient, abundant" were the sole standard for all "value" in the world, there would be no room for any complicated judgment. Everything would be neatly settled by this alone.

And yet the human being, the "agent" that sets the things of this world in motion, makes judgments by a rather different set of values, chooses accordingly, and sometimes even stakes his life on that choice. "Good," "beautiful," "interesting," "adorable," "drawn to it." These are values that can't be compared in numbers or symbols, that resist even being defined, and in the actual world they are always being asked about, actively, restlessly.

No matter how hard you try to "put it into words (into symbols)," the most you can do is trace a faint outline around the value. It stays in the realm of "I don't really get it, but it's just so wonderful!"

We used to name this kind of "value" art and fence it off separately, but it also belongs to the territory of "chic" and "cool." I don't understand it, but things are getting interesting around here!
