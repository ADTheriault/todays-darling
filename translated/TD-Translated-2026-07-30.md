## Title: "Let's do what we can" still holds true today.
## Date: 2026-07-30
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

No matter what happens, the answer is always: "Let's do what we can." Emotions may hurry us along, may leave us unsettled, but eventually, the "things each of us can do" should slowly come into view. The help we need right away, the assistance required along the way, and the long, patient support. All of it is necessary, together with a feeling of prayer.

Well then, today too, I'll paste in yesterday's little scraps of notes.

Even though I own so many nail clippers, I've stepped down, for now, from that whole nail-clipper connoisseur game. Why? Because I've started using a glass nail file. Being able to care for your nails without any blade at all, it's really something. Come to think of it, some villainous boss in an old movie used a nail file too. So, heh, maybe I'm a villainous boss myself now...

I came up with my own eternal phrase. "There are all kinds of people. It can't be helped." I've genuinely come to feel this way. By "all kinds" I mean good and bad, right and wrong, every last bit of it. You can't dye all these different people into a single color, and you mustn't try. On top of accepting that, you team up, you give up, you find your separate spaces to live. Nature became the nature we know now precisely through this process.

Apparently a new series of "Shinya Shokudo" is starting again this autumn. It began in 2009, and it's been seven years since the last one. I love that the staff and cast are reuniting. Yesterday, Kaoru Kobayashi came by Hobonichi and talked with us about "Shinya Shokudo," complete with plenty of idle chatter.

That feeling of "I want to work with that team" has grown much larger for me than all sorts of other conditions. I heard that an amazing crew has gathered for the "Chiikawa" film, and what that means is, it became a team where the doers show up, the ones thinking "I want to work alongside people like that." I think any kind of work heads in this direction. Thank you for coming to Hobonichi today, too. I'd like to think of the blisteringly hot days and the merely somewhat hot days as two separate things.
