## Title: Hey, are you being interviewed out there?!
## Date: 2026-10-16
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Every day, I've been keeping up something I call the "reader interview." Those of you who get it probably already get it, but this isn't what you'd call a survey. A survey is when the person asking poses whatever questions happen to suit their own purposes. What I'm doing is meant to be an interview. I announced that I'd keep going for a hundred questions, and we're already past seventy, so it'll all wrap up in about a month.

You grow up in the ordinary way, live in the ordinary way, and grow old in the ordinary way. In the lives most people lead, there aren't many occasions for anything like an "interview." Maybe a few, but I doubt anyone gets asked a hundred questions. Most of us do get asked things, of course, but only when the person asking needs something from us, or has some reason to ask. Nobody really asks you about things that don't much matter. At a matchmaking meeting, say, or between two people who want to get to know each other better, there might be some curious back-and-forth, but ask too many questions and it starts to feel a bit rude...

But what I'm doing this time is downright relentless. It's a long-form interview with each of you, one by one. Even if you were the guest on some TV special or in a magazine feature, you'd never be interviewed at this length. And on top of that, I, the interviewer, am not asking because I'm especially dying to know. I'm keeping these interviews going so that you can meet the you who goes looking for answers. And then, if you'd like, you can write it down in an email and send it my way. The real event, though, is the time you spend thinking.

Of course, I read the emails too. A picture starts to emerge of a certain person giving a certain answer, which is fascinating, and I even get the feeling I'm being of some use. I think this is a pretty unusual experiment. Are you enjoying the fun of meeting yourself?

Today's question, no. 073: "What question would you like to be asked?" This one too, if you'd like, please write it up and send it to me by email.

Thank you, as always, for stopping by Hobonichi today. "People grow by being interviewed": that's a pet theory of mine.
