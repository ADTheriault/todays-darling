## Title: Not that I don't badmouth people myself
## Date: 2026-11-20
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

When someone talks trash about another person without holding back, nothing really happens as long as they're doing it alone. Even if two or three people are badmouthing someone to their hearts' content, if it never reaches the person being talked about, it probably doesn't go so far as to hurt anyone. And if, in some corner of some region of Japan, the locals were cursing somebody up and down, I doubt it would have had much of an effect either. "Oh, him? He's got a bad name around here." That sort of thing happens all the time.

Now suppose that at some point, somewhere, there was a big wall, and everyone started writing insults on it. Maybe they'd even give it a name, something like "the Badmouth Wall." Suddenly the trash talk that used to pass between two or three people can be read by everyone who walks by. "Go over there and you can read the dirt on somebody." Before long it turns into a kind of entertainment in rather poor taste, and it might even become a tourist attraction of the darker sort. Whoever scrawls the juiciest insults, the ones most likely to catch people's interest, could end up a dark hero of sorts, the toast of the town. True or false, clean or filthy, it doesn't matter. Badmouthing has impact, and there's something in it that draws people in.

Even I, sitting here writing this, have to admit there are insults I'd go and read with a rather unsavory "Ooh, let's have a look." I'm especially drawn to trash talk about people I don't care for, or about people who dislike me.

In the world since the internet took hold, the Badmouth Wall has become a business, and a place where people throw stones to blow off steam, where you can write just about anything you like. Even when it's about someone I dislike, I often find myself thinking, "You can really say that?!" Is it really all right for people to be badmouthed so freely? I don't think it is, not even when it's someone I can't stand, or an enemy. The internet has become an unpleasant place, I think, and all I can do is resolve that, at the very least, I won't take part in it myself.
