## Title: Because there should be plenty of "subjectivity," too.
## Date: 2026-08-15
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I suspect that, for a while now, I'll be thinking and writing more and more about this one thing. It's the matter of "subjectivity."

Every day lately, as an ongoing series of "interviews with readers," I've been tossing out all sorts of questions. What I'm asking about always has something to do with that person's own subjective experience.

Well, to give an example: once I finish writing this piece today, I'll ask, "When was the last time you said goodbye?" Everyone, I imagine, says goodbye quite casually. An answer like "Oh, I just said it to so-and-so a moment ago" is perfectly fine. Some people might recall, "Come to think of it, I rarely go out of my way to actually say goodbye." And there may be someone who thinks, "It's not the goodbyes I've said that I remember, but the ones said to me." In other words, you picture something that actually happened to you.

And then, when you're specifically asked about it, you wonder which one to answer with, or you think, this one came to mind but I'll keep it to myself. You take something only you know, weigh it for yourself, and write it down for your own sake, or send it off in an email. There's no correct answer, it isn't a survey. You just think about what you thought, and jot it down. What lies at the root of it all is the memory of the life you've lived.

It's simple. Anyone who tries can do it, shallowly or deeply as they like. Slipping away from the question entirely is your own prerogative too. All you have to do is think and write, just as your subjectivity leads you.

But this is something "AI" cannot do. On the level of text, it may go through the exchange of saying "goodbye," but it's only saying it in a scene where "goodbye" is called for. It doesn't say goodbye because it wants to say goodbye. It's reproducing the form in which humans do these things.

As the situations where subjectivity is hard to voice keep multiplying, subjectivity turns to mist and slips out of sight. I want us to hold on more firmly to the fact that subjectivity exists. You, the person you are, are a you who possesses a subjectivity belonging to no one else.

◆So, yes. It's no. 011, "When was the last time you said goodbye?" Shall we take even just five minutes to remember, and give it some thought? If you'd like, please write it in an email and send it my way.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today.
