## Title: "Chiikawa" has few words but tons of facial expression.
## Date: 2026-08-11
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

As always, I've been thinking of nothing but "Chiikawa." The news keeps reporting that box office receipts have blown past 6.7 billion yen, that it's a massive hit, but surely all of that was simply "as expected." Because even with this many people pouring into theaters, there were still enough of the little bonus gifts to go around. Naturally, this is because each cinema had them ready and waiting. Those admission bonuses, the kind people say they'd "gladly pay money for," were manufactured in advance, more than a million of them, delivered to every theater in cardboard boxes and the like, and so they could be handed out. There was none of that frantic, scrambling chaos. If "Chiikawa" were a sports team, I'd find myself admiring what a formidable "defense" it has.

As for the film itself, people are offering their "interpretations" all over the place, so I'll hold back a little on that front.

Why is it that people want to talk about "Chiikawa" so very much? There are all sorts of reasons behind that secret, and I'm sure everyone wants to speak about those too, but the thing I find fascinating is this.

The main characters in "Chiikawa" hardly speak any actual "language" at all. The protagonist, Chiikawa, only ever manages little breathy exhales or small hesitant murmurs, and Usagi does nothing but let out these rallying yelps and nonsense cries, more like a toddler than anything. It's practically Hachiware alone who does the talking in real words. And the fact that that entire world holds together this way leaves the reader "room to imagine an enormous amount of language." Scenes where the characters simply nod at one another, or laugh together, come up again and again, and it just feels right. If the main characters put everything into words, there'd be no need for the reader to pour their own language into the gaps.
