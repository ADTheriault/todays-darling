## Title: What kind of company is Hobonichi, really?
## Date: 2026-11-17
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I wonder how we, the Hobonichi crew, look to people on the outside. I wouldn't be surprised if they took us for a carefree, head-in-the-clouds sort of bunch. The struggles, the gritted teeth, the serious specialist know-how: hardly any of that shows from the outside, so I suppose it can't be helped. Then again, some people imagine the exact opposite. They figure "places like that are the ones that are secretly brutal," and picture us as some kind of ogre's lair. Well, they're free to think so, but I'd say it's neither that harsh a place nor a battlefield. When I say "everyone here is a good person," I honestly mean it, though I doubt anyone takes that at face value.

What Hobonichi is really like is hard to get across in words. I think the people who understand it best are the ones who've come in as part-timers for something like our Joy of Living Fair and worked right alongside us. It's tough, if you want to call it tough, and it's fun, if you want to call it fun. When we say "Safety first, fun second," we mean it. Then again, we also say "Health first, fun second."

Right now we're running a series of articles recruiting interns, under the title "Come along on the Hobonichi journey." New people at Hobonichi get to know the place by working with us. Spend enough time on the inside and you see just about everything, and both sides learn more about each other than any number of interviews and exams could ever reveal.

So here's the thing. As part of the reading material on that recruitment page, there's a set of crew interviews. Eight have been posted so far. Young people who joined as interns themselves sit down with other former interns, and with senior colleagues who came in through other doors. They make for really fun reading, and they feel true to life. To be honest, even I, the longest-serving member of the crew, have been enjoying them as a reader. Which makes sense, I suppose, given that the series is called "Honestly, what was hard about joining Hobonichi?"

If you're the parent, or an older friend or mentor, of someone who might apply for the internship, please feel free to let them know about it. And I'd be glad to get emails with your thoughts on this piece, too. Thanks.

Thank you for stopping by Hobonichi today. My cold has turned into the real thing, so I'm taking the day off work.
