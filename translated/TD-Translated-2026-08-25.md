## Title: I haven't given up on my "Chiikawa calligraphy"
## Date: 2026-08-25
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I've never been any good at lessons, the kind you practice at. I've given up on so many of them over the years. I don't know why exactly, but they genuinely wear me down. Memorizing the steps, the lines, the choreography, I just can't do it. When I was young I thought this was something I ought to overcome, but now I'm thoroughly grown up, and once I started doing things as part of a team, we realized it was better to divide the work by what each person was good at rather than forcing yourself through what you weren't. So I threw myself into the things I was good at, and left the things I struggled with to other people.

I assumed I'd just keep going that way. But lately I've been earnestly doing the very thing I was always bad at. It's called rinsho: you look at a model and reproduce the flow of the brush. It's a practice repeated endlessly in the world of calligraphy. The other day I started my own version, "Chiikawa rinsho." I copy the characters that the creator, Nagano-san, draws.

With what motion of the brush, in what shape, in what position, at what size is this little one drawn? When I move my hand trying to draw it the same way, little by little, some things start to come clear. And if I can get the technique into my hands, I should be able to draw something close to a real "Chiikawa."

The original manga is drawn by hand by Nagano-sensei, but so much of it has been adapted into animation as secondary work that there must be some "rule" governing these shapes made of lines and dots. The animators have mastered it, which is how they can both reproduce the original art and make it move. In a sense it's different from the original drawings, and yet within the range that satisfies the viewer, it is more than sufficiently "Chiikawa."

I don't need to get all that good at it. But I'd love to draw a "Chiikawa" that seems to have just welled up onto the sketchbook page on its own. I hope I can get that far. I do a little rinsho every day, but the road looks long.
