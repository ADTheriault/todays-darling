## Title: Shibuya Seibu will be gone next month.
## Date: 2026-09-20
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I decided to go to the Shibuya Seibu to buy some Turkish sweets made with pistachio. Really, I was using that as an excuse. I wanted to see the Shibuya Seibu one more time before it closed. I'd had a vague sense that it would be gone sometime this autumn, but only then did I learn the actual date: Wednesday, September 30th. It might well have vanished without my even noticing. Somewhere along the way, it had quietly become that sort of thing to me.

I did a little shopping, wandered across a few floors, took in the corridors and walls and stairways, and tried to savor the sort of commonplace sentimentality you'd expect. But it wasn't especially interesting. "How could things have gone differently, and turned out better?" I found myself thinking, like some half-baked executive. More than sadness, what welled up in me was the vexed feeling of facing an unsolvable problem.

Outsiders and critics can say whatever they please, but who could really know where, and how, and what might have led to what other outcome? Successes and unmistakable failures, all tangled together, winding their way to this point. Over the years there must have been countless earnest people in charge, countless clever strategists, cycling in and out.

And beyond that, I can't even say whether this "closing" on September 30th is truly a sad thing at all. I'm in no position to pretend I understand. At some point in the nineteenth century the department store was born, changed with the times, grew larger, and has come down to us in its present form. Even if department stores gradually change and eventually disappear, the fact that "people found joy in them" surely won't vanish. The joy of the people who gathered there, that was there, might it not have shifted somewhere else, into some other form, and be shining there still, even if not in the shape of a department store? I turn these things over in my mind, but honestly I don't really understand any of it. I find myself imagining, of all things, our own "Life's Little Pleasures" exhibition, but then again...
