## Title: Now I know about biofilm
## Date: 2026-11-16
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

A few years ago I came across the word "biofilm," and I can't help wondering how many people know it. It sounds kind of cool, doesn't it? "Bio"! "Film"! It sounds like some hot new product. You can just picture someone saying, "Oh, out on the cutting edge, everyone's gone biofilm now." That's not what it is, though.

Biofilm is the slimy stuff on your teeth. It's also the slime in your kitchen drain, and the slick coating on river rocks, the kind that sends your foot shooting right out from under you. Leave a water bottle unwashed and you'll get biofilm in there too. We already had perfectly familiar ways of describing it, the film on your teeth, the gunk in the drain, the slippery stones, and yes, that's the stuff. Apparently it's a state in which bacteria gather together and live as a community, all influencing one another. So, not cool at all. Kind of disgusting, really. It turns out bacteria find life easier in a group than on their own, so they set up house together inside that sticky structure. Gross, right? Once you know, you get a lot more serious about brushing. I brush every morning and every night now as if I'm heading into battle against biofilm.

It seems people discovered microorganisms living in the grime on teeth a whole 340 years ago, but the idea of "biofilm" only caught on in the 1970s. Can you believe it, my dear? Back when I was drawing posters for Cavity Prevention Day, I pictured germs in little devil costumes swinging pickaxes, pounding holes into teeth like they were making rice cakes. And the teacher was perfectly happy with that explanation, too. No bio, no film, nothing of the sort.

Ever since I learned about it, biofilm has been on my mind. And now that you've read this, you know too, so from here on out you'll be living in a world with biofilm in it. Sorry about that. I really didn't need to do that to you.
