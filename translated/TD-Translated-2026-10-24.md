## Title: The Hobonichi Techo his mother kept
## Date: 2026-10-24
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I'd taken a taxi for a ride of a little under thirty minutes, and just before I got out, the driver spoke up, very hesitantly. "I'm sorry to bother you like this. It's just that there's something I really wanted to tell you..." The courtesy of that preamble had me answering "Yes?" before I knew it.

"She's passed away now, but my mother was a huge fan of the Hobonichi Techo. For years and years she kept a diary in it, and she treasured it. We still have them all, and I wanted to say thank you."

No one had ever said anything like that to me before, and I couldn't think how to respond. "Oh, well, thank you so much. I'm glad it was of use to her," I said, and then went and asked something that wasn't really my business: "How old was your mother?"

"She was seventy-two. Oh, there are so many memories in those planners. I just really wanted to thank you."

Gladness got tangled up with the search for some words of condolence, and I climbed out of the taxi mumbling something halting like, "Oh, I see. Well, well..."

It's funny. People have been pleased by things I've made over the years, games, song lyrics, all sorts of things. But being thanked for a planner by the son of a woman who lived past seventy and is no longer with us? That was probably a first. I've been making the Hobonichi Techo for a long time now, and somewhere along the way it has come to connect families across a few generations.

Come to think of it, there was a time at a sushi restaurant when the family in the next seats showed me a brand-new Hobonichi Techo. "We gave it to our daughter just today, to celebrate her starting junior high." That girl might well be old enough by now to have a daughter of her own.
