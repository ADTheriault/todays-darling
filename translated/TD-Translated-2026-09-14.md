## Title: This is the fun part.
## Date: 2026-09-14
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Surprise and familiarity. It seems we're drawn to things that have both.

Not the things we can more or less see coming, but the ones that make us wonder, where did this come from? or, why on earth is this happening? The kind of thing that catches us off guard. In short, surprise.

When something follows a clear, logical progression, this leads to that, and then you get this, there's no real fun in it. But then, hmm, how should I put it, there's this quality of familiarity, a sense that the thing settles in comfortably beside you. It surprises you, yes, but you can tell it isn't your enemy. And before you've even started to like it, some warmth of affection is already drifting your way from its direction. This part matters just as much.

Surprise and familiarity might seem a little contradictory, but there are countless things that manage to hold both at once. Think back on the things you love, the people you love, the things you like to do. Try calling all sorts of them to mind.

With only surprise, you could say it becomes a bit frightening. A little suspense is fine, but too much of it makes you want to keep your distance, I think. And with only familiarity, the joy inevitably fades. It won't necessarily bore you, but you find yourself wanting more play in it, if that makes sense.

Everyone has their own tastes and quirks and chemistry, so I can't say there's one universally good answer. Still, there really are things that make a great many people feel that mix of surprise and familiarity.

Well, as you all know, these days I've been thinking quite a lot about Chiikawa, haven't I? And that world is built from a mountain's worth of surprise and an ocean's worth of familiarity.
