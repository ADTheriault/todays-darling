## Title: This short piece was "Today's Darling."
## Date: 2026-08-26
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

This piece I write every day was originally called "Today's Darling." The joke was in getting people to go out of their way to call someone like me "Darling." I mean, "It doesn't suit me at all, does it? Heh heh heh." But little by little it stuck, and time went on, and new people kept arriving, until eventually we reached an age of "What's a Darling?" So I've been quietly trying to retire the name.

After all, the recent newcomers to Hobonichi didn't even know who Led Zeppelin was. They'd say things like, "I gave them a listen, and they're actually pretty good." Soon enough, everyone will be someone who's never heard of Shigesato Itoi either, and "What's this Darling business?" will be the natural order of things. So we've got to keep changing things up.

Which is why this daily piece I'm writing right now doesn't have a settled name. When I send in the manuscript, I, the person actually writing it, still just tentatively file it under "Today's Darling."

The feeling that I need to change is something I carry with me always. And yet I'm also the kind of guy who wants to keep feeling, always, the texture of the things that don't change.

So we started this daily series, remember, where we interview readers with the invitation, "Write to us by email if you'd like." Reading these every day is genuinely one of my great pleasures. There really are all sorts of people out there. Some of them answer like it's a survey, just a curt "Yes" and nothing more, but that too is part of "everyone." It really makes you feel, deeply, that each person has their own "LIFE."

The stories about fathers and mothers especially, the love and the hatred and everything in between, they were something to read. It's astonishing, the "connection" that runs between parent and child. Sometimes envious, sometimes silently applauding in my heart, I've been reading through so many of them, as the interviewer.

As an aside, the characters in "Chiikawa," every one of them, they're solitary beings with no family. Small, cute, and standing on their own two feet. That too gets to me, right in the chest.

◆Now then, today's a big one. No. 022, "How are you doing?" Ask yourself again, really. And if you feel like it, write to me by email and let me read your answer.

Thank you again for coming to Hobonichi today. Around the office right now, with the release of the Hobonichi Techo coming up, everyone's in a wonderful, spinning frenzy.
