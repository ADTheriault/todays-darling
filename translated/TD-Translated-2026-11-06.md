## Title: Well, let's have some tea and chat...
## Date: 2026-11-06
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Whenever someone says, "Not to brag or anything, but...", there's usually at least a little bragging mixed in. Or am I wrong?

People say, "We hope all you folks at home will enjoy this," but where exactly are all these homes, I wonder. I'm sure they exist somewhere. I'd just love, one time, to gather them all in one place and take a good look.

Oh, right. Not to brag or anything, but in this precious life of mine, I try never to go without something made with anko, that sweet red bean paste. Sometimes it's this or that from Toraya, sometimes Akafuku from Ise, sometimes Hanazono Manju from Shinjuku, sometimes the hot-spring buns from Seihotei in Ikaho, sometimes dorayaki from Usagiya in Ueno, sometimes dorayaki from Kamejyu in Asakusa, sometimes mame daifuku from Mizuho in Harajuku, sometimes mame daifuku from Matsushimaya in Takanawa, sometimes taiyaki from Naniwaya in Azabu-Juban, sometimes anpan from Kimuraya in Ginza, sometimes Yamadaya buns from Uwa, sometimes bamboo-leaf dumplings from Tanakaya in Niigata, sometimes Mosuke dango from Toyosu... and there are plenty more, but those are the ones that spring to mind right away.

Some I can pick up nearby, some require a little outing, some I bring back as souvenirs, some are gifts, some I order by mail. On top of that, I sometimes make anko myself. In any case, life without red bean sweets would be a real problem.

The other day, though, I learned that Kashiwaya in Fukushima, makers of the thin-skinned buns I adore, had switched to packaging that keeps longer, so I bought a whole batch. And because they were reliably there every single day, my gratitude faded, and they stopped thrilling me the way they used to. I've been reflecting on that.

Even in days so devoted to red bean sweets, there comes the occasional day when I simply want a Western-style cake. So today I bought a strawberry shortcake and ate it.

Today's question, no. 094: "Go ahead, write whatever you like." Not sure what to say? If you feel like it, write it in an email and send it our way.

Thanks for stopping by Hobonichi again today. You know, the frozen imagawayaki they sell at convenience stores are surprisingly good, too.
