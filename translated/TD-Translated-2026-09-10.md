## Title: It's not that I don't think about happiness.
## Date: 2026-09-10
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Happiness, unhappiness, these aren't things you can speak of easily. I can't even claim to understand my own. To actually say the words out loud, "Ah, I'm so happy," well, that's the sort of thing that only happens in the Kayama Yuzo songs of a certain era, isn't it? No, no, you're allowed to say it, please, say it as much as you like. That's all fine. But there's a theory that goes, "People who are inside happiness don't stop to think about whether they're happy or not," and I remember feeling somehow convinced by it. When someone makes a special point of saying, "I'm happy right now," it feels to me like they're comparing it against something else, something that isn't happiness at all.

If you asked a child playing in the park, gloriously absorbed in nothing, "Are you happy right now?" they might answer "Happy!" without a second thought, but I doubt the idea had even crossed their mind.

The truth is, happiness and unhappiness are a kind of abstract "measuring stick" for how we feel, and people go on living without ever reaching for that stick. Whether a doorway is eighty centimeters wide, or ninety, you'd never think about it in the ordinary course of your life. It's only when it becomes relevant that you start thinking about doors in numbers, "All right, let's go with eighty centimeters." For most of the time we're alive, people should be able to get by without measuring or verifying their happiness. To put it the other way around, it's precisely because we drag out that "measuring stick" of happiness that we end up meeting its flip side, "unhappiness."

And yet, thinking about what happiness actually is, about what constitutes unhappiness and how to escape it, human history has gone on pondering these questions the whole way through. And to be honest, I've done my share of pondering too. But the more I think about it, the more tangled it all becomes. To the point where I catch myself wishing the very concept of happiness didn't exist at all.
