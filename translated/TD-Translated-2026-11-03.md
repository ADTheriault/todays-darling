## Title: More on the reader interviews, and other things
## Date: 2026-11-03
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

The "reader interview" I've been running at the end of these short pieces is getting close to its hundredth question. Which is to say, that's where it ends. After doing it a hundred days in a row, I have to admit it's a little sad to see it go. Especially for me, you know. The interview takes up three lines, so I'd been trimming the writing that came before it. At first I couldn't get used to that, but little by little I did, and along the way I picked up a "somewhat freer" way of writing. It's probably hard for anyone else to notice, but I'd loosened up a bit. If I go back to the original twenty-seven characters by forty lines, I suspect I'll struggle with that in its own way.

Now, there's something I should tell you ahead of time. I'd planned to stop at question 100, on that very day, but I've decided to keep the interview going for one more. The reason is that no. 074, "Is there a place you're a regular at?", and no. 040, "Is there a place you always go back to?", were far too similar. I thought I'd been careful to come up with questions that didn't overlap with ones I'd already asked, but I slipped up. Truth is, I ask similar things all the time. Then again, that happens plenty even when you're talking face to face with someone, so I'd been letting it slide. This time, though, I wanted a do-over. So question 101 will run on November 13th, and that will be the end.

I'll probably talk about this project again once it wraps up, but interviews really are curious occasions, aren't they? There are things you'd never have thought about if nobody had asked, and sometimes being asked leads you to discover something about yourself. I've been on both sides, asking and being asked, and even now I often find myself stopping short and thinking, "Huh, so that's how I saw it."

And then there's something Satoru Iwata used to say a lot, too: "Once it's written down, you end up sounding like you're full of yourself." There's that tendency, and I really can't stand it. The more honestly and plainly you answer, the more likely you are to come off sounding cocky. I've even been known to ask for my blunt sentence endings to be swapped out for polite ones, you know (ha).
