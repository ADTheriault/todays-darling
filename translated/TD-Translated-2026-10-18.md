## Title: A modest case for copying
## Date: 2026-10-18
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I want to write about something I've kept up for a while now, my "Chiikawa rinsho."

To begin with, I've come to think that rinsho is a fascinating thing. In calligraphy, the word means copying a model by hand. Nearly every form of creative expression is dogged by the idea that originality is what matters most. But rinsho starts from an existing model and, in a sense, expresses it all over again. To someone who puts individuality above everything, that probably makes no sense at all. And yet in the world of calligraphy, the great historical hands, the masters and virtuosos whose names have come down through the centuries, all practiced rinsho endlessly. Watching from the corner of my eye, I'd always sensed there must be something deep and complicated going on there.

A few things came to mind. Maybe it's a bit like performing classical music. Then there's Tadanori Yokoo, who keeps insisting, "My paintings are copies," "They're all copies of reality." There's a book by Takaaki Yoshimoto called Imitation and the Mirror, a title I always thought was terribly cool. And there was my long-standing interest in why impressions and mimicry are so much fun. Things like that.

Then it struck me that Nagano's Chiikawa drawings feel like both calligraphy and pictures at once, and I decided to try rinsho with them as my model. I'd done something similar back in junior high. I loved the Chōjū-giga animal scrolls and tried drawing copies of them, and I drew the characters from Fujio Akatsuka's manga too. It felt as though I could make something of value with my own two hands, and that made me surprisingly happy.

I bought art supplies right away, named the project "Chiikawa Rinsho," and got started. There's a pleasure in it not unlike cuddling a stuffed animal, and the more you draw, the more a kind of reverent affection for the model grows in you. Somewhere along the way, you begin to feel you're tracing what the artist was thinking, what they were careful about, what felt good to them as they drew. So far I'm not the least bit tired of it. I haven't gotten any better, either.
