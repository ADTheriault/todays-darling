## Title: Hey everyone, are you fired up and starting something?
## Date: 2026-09-05
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

"Recovery and restoration are not the same thing." I said this many times, back when rubble still lay scattered everywhere, talking with the people of Kesennuma.

The disaster took so much away, and putting things back the way they had been, that is restoration. There are records and memories of what once stood there, and you can roughly gauge what needs to be done. Budgets get assigned, plans for restoration are drawn up, and carried through to completion. It matters enormously. Thanks to this work of restoration, the disaster areas of that time have become what they are now.

And yet, I have a feeling that neither I nor the people living there ever quite understood what "recovery," as distinct from restoration, was really supposed to be.

For a region whose past has been swept away by earthquake and tsunami, it is easy enough to "say" that it will seize the moment and "recover." In Japanese, only a single character changes between the two words, while the character meaning "to return" stays the same. But that one new character is not so simple.

It is a remarkable character, really. It carries a great deal on its shoulders. In its ancient oracle bone form, it seems to have depicted several pairs of hands lifting something up together. It holds a dynamic sense of "raising up," of "getting something going."

That same character appears in words for promotion, for the rise of something new, for enterprise, for staging a performance. And it reaches further still, into the movements of the heart: interest, fascination, taking delight in something, being carried away by the moment, having one's mood spoiled, excitement, and so on. There is a sense of being lifted up and set into motion, don't you think?

So it was that character, the one that "raises up," and not the one that simply "returns," that we wanted. When we said, back then, that we wanted not restoration but recovery, I wonder just how much of it has actually come to pass. It is only now, at this late date, that I find myself wanting to think it over again.

And here is another thing. People talk about "Japan mired in stagnation," and they have been saying such things for decades now. But here too, is there anywhere that something is being "raised up," anywhere that something is genuinely "rising"? I would like to go looking for that spirit of "raising up," while taking a hard look at myself as well.

Founding a company, launching an industry, these too are expressed with that very same character.
