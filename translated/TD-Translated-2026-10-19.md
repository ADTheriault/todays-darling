## Title: The health class that really matters
## Date: 2026-10-19
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Once in a while, I suppose I ought to say something befitting a man of my years.

This is about "health and PE," like the subject you had in school: your health and your body. When you work freelance, or in some arrangement close to it, things like regular checkups, dental exams and vaccinations start to feel like a hassle, and they quietly slide. "I'm so healthy I haven't seen a doctor in ten years," someone will say, and no doubt it's meant as a boast. Good health is the best thing there is, of course, but... Once you've racked up a certain number of years, even the sturdiest body starts to have a few things worth keeping an eye on.

For my part, shortly before I turned fifty, a fellow business owner a little senior to me invited me to get a full medical checkup, and we did the overnight program together. He never said anything preachy, but I took it as a lesson: a company president's health is a responsibility, not just his own business. I was never exactly reckless, but somewhere in me was the feeling that fussing over your own body wasn't the proper, devil-may-care way of a true Tokyoite. Never mind that I was actually born up north in Gunma.

That first checkup got me thinking of caring for my body and health as one of my important jobs. Quitting smoking and going to the gym regularly are part of that same kind of work. Without it, I don't think I'd have the health I have now. Even if I were still freelance today, "health and PE" would be a crucial investment in myself, my one and only worker. Skimp on your own basic costs and you can't do good work.

Somewhere past forty, people start swapping stories about their ailments. You've still got a long road ahead of you, so instead of cracking self-deprecating jokes, go do your "health and PE." All the more so if you have a spouse or young children. If you've got your health, you can do anything, as the saying goes. But health costs something too.
