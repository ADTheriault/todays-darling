## Title: On my silly recent circumstances.
## Date: 2026-09-27
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

I've been writing this "Today's Darling (as it was formerly called)" every single day, and I've never once forgotten to write it. Before I fall asleep, I make absolutely certain to finish it, and only then do I go to bed.

What's a little embarrassing to admit, though, is that I do sometimes forget to send in the piece I've written. It happens a few times a year, and I'll get a phone call in the morning. To make the eleven o'clock update, someone will say, "We haven't received your manuscript."

The thing is, the piece is right there on my desk. All that's left is to send it, so I say sorry, sorry, and fire it off immediately. Actually, when you've been doing this for over twenty-eight years, there turn out to be all sorts of patterns to forgetting to send in your work, but I'll spare you the details.

And so, well, yesterday brought a first for me: a case where the piece itself simply hadn't been written.

I had dealt with the more familiar "we haven't received it," and, thoroughly reassured, was yawning and thinking I might drift back to sleep, when a second prompt arrived, delicious as a fresh dashi poured over the first: "There's no reader interview at the end."

Ah, that might be true, yes, that was it!

Lately there have been a string of days off, and since my daytime schedule has been my own, I've been able to do things like write the next day's "Today's Darling (as it was formerly called)" while it's still light out. If I write it ahead of time, I can go about my evening and late night with room to spare, and even turn in early if I like. Wanting that sense of ease, I write it in the bright of the afternoon, and then I can relax, thinking, "I don't have to write anything more today."

Relax a little too much, and I forget to send it. That happens too. What's more, I'd left the "question" part blank, telling myself I'd think it over later at my leisure. You fool, you fool! That blank space was the true culprit.

This "reader interview" is something I set out to do one hundred times. I don't think it's fine to stop at fifty-two. And I myself find real pleasure in tossing out a question each day and receiving the answers. For just a moment I nearly thought, "Maybe I'll take today off," but no, that won't do, I told myself right away, and posted it, late.

◆Today's question is no. 054, "What would you do with 5,000 yen?" (No. 015 was 500 million yen.) Please write your answer in an email and send it to us.

Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today.

Sometimes it's only when someone asks that you finally understand something about yourself.
