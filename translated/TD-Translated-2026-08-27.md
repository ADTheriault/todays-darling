## Title: What it means to have "baka" attached
## Date: 2026-08-27
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

"That Itoi, he's a good-natured fool, honest to the point of stupidity." The one they were saying this about wasn't me, it was my father. He'd often sit drinking and talking with his close friends. That phrase, "good-natured to the point of stupidity," was, in its way, a bit of affectionate name-calling among people who liked each other. They'd even say things like, "He's a fool, a fool, a great big fool." And my father, on the receiving end, didn't seem to mind at all. If anything, he sat there grinning, thoroughly pleased with himself. Even then, I could sense it was a kind of backhanded praise.

Maybe the reason I remember it all so vividly is that the same scene played out over and over, again and again. To me as a child, though, the word "fool" seemed to be getting thrown around a little too conveniently, and I have to admit it wasn't a conversation I enjoyed.

Good-natured to the point of being a fool, honest to the point of being a fool. In a certain sense, isn't that an "invincible" banner to wave? A banner that cancels out all sorts of other, less flattering qualities. If it were me today, I might well have cut in with, "Come on, nobody's actually like that."

Later, once I was no longer a child, a manga called "Karate Baka Ichidai" ran in serialization, and, well, I read it avidly. But the way "fool" is used there is a little sly too, isn't it? If everyone else is "not a fool," then this particular "fool" starts to look like something good, something worth protecting. And I believe it was Fujio Akatsuka who came up with it, but Yuya Uchida's nickname, "the rock 'n' roll fool," has that same effect of neutralizing everything else about a person.

"Fool," as long as you're the one claiming it for yourself, is probably a wonderfully convenient word, like a crown you place on your own head. And that's precisely because it's a word you'd hate to have aimed at you. He who willingly dons the "crown of the fool" shall be revered. Not that I have any intention of going and doing that myself.
