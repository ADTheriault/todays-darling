## Title: I couldn't have done it on my own, but.
## Date: 2026-08-16
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

You tag along with a parent and end up experiencing something. It can be anything, really. Fishing, traveling, cooking, working the fields, digging a hole in the ground. Whatever it is, it becomes an experience. It's not something a child could do alone, but through it you learn, "Ah, so this is something that can be done." Just having done it once broadens your sense of what's possible, gives you the feeling that "this isn't beyond me."

I must have been not quite twenty at the time. My father, born in the eighth year of the Taisho era, rode on an airplane for the very first time. He seemed genuinely moved, thinking, "Now this is really something." So he told me to go and ride a plane too. He'd cover the cost, he said, so I should fly somewhere, anywhere. I suspect the idea of actually arranging it all himself and putting me on the plane felt too daunting, so he didn't want to go that far.

As for me, I was a provincial high school student in the 1960s who had never even set foot in an airport. Buying my own boarding pass and getting on a plane was something I simply didn't imagine I could do. It just wasn't in my world. And so my father's "go ride a plane" quietly faded away and came to nothing.

Children alive today must be different. Most of them have probably flown before, and the adults around them have all flown too, so they already know that getting on a plane "isn't beyond them." That difference, I think, is a fairly significant one.

My father, I imagine, had few experiences of doing something, of managing something, with the help of another person. It was a different era, and he hadn't been raised in wealth. So the world of things he could picture himself doing, the realm of "ah, so this can be done," was probably quite small. And I, raised by that father, followed his example. I had a strong streak of "I'll figure it out on my own."

The things you experience with the help of a parent or someone else get stored away in your memory as "things I'll probably be able to do myself someday." Looking back on my own life, I find that rather enviable. Doing everything from scratch, with nothing but your own two hands, isn't something you should choose on purpose.
