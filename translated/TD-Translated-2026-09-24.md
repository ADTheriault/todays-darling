## Title: Thoughts while gazing out at the endless rain.
## Date: 2026-09-24
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

This year there seems to be an awful lot of rain. I haven't been keeping track or counting the days, but it feels like I'm worrying about rain more than in a usual summer or a usual autumn. Then again, I've said the phrase "the long rains of autumn" plenty of times before, so maybe this is just what autumn is normally like. I can't say for sure.

With the long weekend, not seeing anyone from the office and staying in because of the rain, I catch myself thinking, so this is what the life of a hermit must be like. I could just go out, rain or wind or typhoon and all. It's not as if there's nothing to do at home. There's endless things to do.

But the things you ought to do get pushed aside, and instead you find yourself wanting to knock out the things you don't need to do first. You go looking for those unnecessary things with surprising seriousness. Sometimes that means "making fried rice," or, as I've done to exhaustion in the past, "making jam."

Right now I have this thing I call "Chiikawa calligraphy practice," so whenever it occurs to me I could just do that. But once a small daily pleasure starts becoming a good habit, it gets "promoted" into something I have to do. And so I end up hunting for even more things I don't need to do.

I watch films and dramas I'd figured I could skip. The ones I've saved up thinking "I'm definitely going to watch this" I want to watch properly, so the ones I can start half-heartedly are the films I'd more or less decided I didn't really need to see. The same goes for books. When there's a book I've settled on as "this looks interesting, I'm absolutely reading it," I end up starting on some other book I'd bought almost as an afterthought beforehand.

Over the course of these long rains, there have been quite a lot of not-the-favorite dramas and books I read on a whim. I watched a fair bit of the Asian Games, too. I even took up "sudoku," which I'd never touched before. And all of them, each in its own way, you could say were interesting enough. Maybe it's the ones we don't think of as "the favorite" that we end up growing close to.
