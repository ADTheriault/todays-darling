## Title: To my younger self, even if it sounds like a lecture
## Date: 2026-10-11
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

There are some things you come to understand quite naturally once you've grown old. One of them is noticing how remarkable other people's efforts are, and being able to honor and respect them.

You know those long stone staircases leading up to temples and shrines in the mountains, the ones that seem to go on forever? You climb them breathlessly, stopping to rest every so often, just barely making it. And even though climbing them is hard enough, you start to imagine the people who cleared that path and built those steps in the first place. How did they carve out the route? How did they haul the stone and timber up there? How did they shape it all into stairs? And it doesn't end there. These steps exist, after all, for the temple or shrine standing at the very top. A staggering amount of labor was gathered together in this one place. Even with the machines and tools we have today, it isn't something that could be done overnight.

Even if I can't fully picture it, being able to let my thoughts wander to such an enormous undertaking and feel a sense of reverence is something that, in my case, only became possible once I'd finally become an old man. And it isn't just stone steps. I find myself feeling this way about all sorts of things.

If I'd had this kind of respectful imagination back in my thirties, say, I think my life would have turned out quite differently. Then again, you could also say it was precisely because I lacked that kind of imagination that I was able to make my fearless declarations, throw myself into reckless adventures, and launch ventures without any real plan.

My life has been like one of those ladder lotteries where you trace a line down and turn at every rung, and there's no going back to draw it again. The good and the bad alike, it's because I turned at those particular corners that I am who I am now, standing where I stand.

Still, you know, noticing how remarkable other people's efforts are is something that's better learned while you're young. So maybe I'll start mentioning it, little by little, to people younger than me. Though if I get too earnest about it, it'll turn into an old man's lecture. Perhaps just writing a little about it here, like this, is about the right amount.
