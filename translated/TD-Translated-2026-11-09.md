## Title: The distance that lets you watch with an easy heart
## Date: 2026-11-09
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

The Bungaku Free Market, the big literary fair at Tokyo Big Sight, is really something, isn't it? It was started by people who thought, "This is what we want to do," and others who felt the same way gathered around them, until it grew into something huge. I suspect they've done it almost entirely without help from professionals. I imagine they've worked hard thinking everything through: if we do it this way, this problem will come up; if that happens, we'll end up with this. But there's no such thing as a perfect event anywhere, so unless something is actually dangerous, it's fine to just do it and fix things as you go. Being an outsider, I can say that sort of thing breezily, without turning into a worrier. I feel the same way about the Hibiya Music Festival that Seiji Kameda and his team have been working so hard on all these years. Watching from a little way off, I can stay calm.

You could call that irresponsible, and maybe it is. But this kind of "watching from a distance" matters. When you're the ones running the show, a certain tension inevitably comes with it, so even with smiles on your faces, the planning and execution tend to come out a bit stiff. That said, most of what Hobonichi does seems to look like we're "just laughing our way through it." I think that's sort of our signature.

This time, the writers of Hobonichi's "3-Minute Column" have been busily pitching in on what amounts to a volunteer basis, and since the venue is only open for a short time, everyone seems relaxed in their own way. This is going to be a good experience for them. They've got a booth, and they made books to sell, but their participation has a slightly hobbyist feel to it. Watching the other booths and the way the organizers run things from that kind of distance must have been a real education. Making, selling, buying, wandering around, keeping watch: whatever role people were in, I bet they were all using their imagination to picture what it's like for those in the other roles.

As for me personally, it was the first time in a while that I spent hours locked in battle with the call of nature.
