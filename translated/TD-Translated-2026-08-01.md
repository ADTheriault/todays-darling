## Title: The real problem you want to solve isn't that one.
## Date: 2026-08-01
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Since this is something I've experienced myself, I can't really speak too harshly about it. But at a certain point in my life, I came to understand this: "When a person is caught up in something to an abnormal, almost obsessive degree, the thing they truly need to resolve is probably something else entirely."

The things a person really wants to resolve, I think, aren't all that many. Relationships close to home, like family or a lover. Social relationships tied to one's pride, like work not going well. Concrete financial troubles, like debt. These are the kinds of things that, if you seriously set out to deal with them, demand a considerable amount of resolve. Getting angry won't help. Grieving won't help. It's hard to find your way to a solution. And even if there's someone willing to lend a hand, the one who has to push through the pain and keep going is you.

"Being fanatically absorbed in something" is a state that radiates energy, and the sight of someone earnestly devoted to a task can look, somehow, like a deeply convincing way to live. So I completely understand throwing yourself into it. And because you're doing it so earnestly, so passionately, it becomes: "I don't have time for anything else."

This is exactly the point. That "anything else" is precisely where "the thing you truly wanted to resolve" was supposed to be all along.

At a certain point, I looked back on my own past self and understood, in hindsight, that this is how it works. The personal, "inner" problems you have to resolve yourself, you try to forget them by placing importance on "outer" problems instead. For lack of a better term, I've come to call this "the externalization of a problem."

Beyond the realm of comfort, entertainment, or play, people pour in their anger, their sorrow, even their health, and sink completely into it. Cases like this, I think, are only increasing in these times.

Thank you again for coming to Hobonichi today. Though I myself used to get called a "fanatical Giants fan."
