## Title: It's not getting old, it's getting seasoned.
## Date: 2026-09-08
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Whether it's a town, a building, a shop, or some much larger landscape, whether it's a team, an organization, a role, or a relationship between people, gradually the "thisness" of the thing soaks in. You know how the broth seeps into the daikon in a pot of oden? It's exactly like that.

A shop that looks like just the sort of place people would love these days, well, ask the right specialist and they'll whip one up in no time. You can get people saying "How lovely!" for a while. But even that "How lovely!" gets grubby and grows old. Before long it becomes a shop that "used to be lovely." And then it might be forgotten, might disappear altogether.

But then there are shops nobody ever calls "lovely," and yet they've been going for thirty years. "The owner's a weird guy," "their potato salad is great," praised in these unremarkable ways, and somehow, thirty years. In a shop like that, the shop's own character has "soaked in."

It's the same with streetscapes, a road that's been there since the Edo period, say. There's something soaked into a place like that. Though when you turn it into nice words like "tradition" or "history," it starts to feel a little off, somehow.

Even a married couple who don't seem all that fond of each other, it might not be splendid, might not be beautiful, but something has soaked into them. A flavor that says, this is us, this pair. There it is.

Business matters too, of course it does. But business on its own can't stand the wait, that "time it takes to soak in." Stylish things, expensive-looking things, they're easy enough to build up and tear down again. But the things that have "soaked in," they can't do without time and something like human sweat.

Well now, Hobonichi hits its thirtieth anniversary two years from now, and I got to thinking, all sorts of good broth has been soaking into it, hasn't it.
