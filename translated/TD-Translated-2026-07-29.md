## Title: On the day of July 28, 2026.
## Date: 2026-07-29
## Author: Shigesato Itoi
## Content:

Every day, all sorts of things happen. Some of them seem, at first glance, to have no bearing on anything else. And yet everything is connected, even if only slightly, to something. Take yesterday, for instance. That was my yesterday.

At "Hobo Station AKAGI," there was a tasting of a certain noodle dish soon to join the menu. Back when it was still just an idea, I offered all kinds of opinions, but today's tasting was at the "probably finished" stage. It's surely delicious, you can tell just by looking at the face of the person who made it. And yes, it was delicious. I want to go all the way to Akagi just to eat it again. I think Mr. Yamaguchi from the Roppongi restaurant has another hit on his hands. I really mean it. I don't hand out flattery like that.

The regular meeting of the "Hobonichi Manga Club." I'd given both myself and the team some homework, so today's gathering had a bit of an edge to it. Something small yet large, humble yet bold, is about to begin. In half a year, the Manga Club has grown in its own way.

I got to speak with Akiomi Hirano of the Taro Okamoto Memorial Museum. He's a thoughtful, intellectual man, but also one who acts with real boldness. He's the one who let "Taroman" run wild, after all. "Taro Okamoto is still interesting even after his death," he says. And that, he believes, is the most important work left to those who remain. "Still interesting even after death." What a wonderful thing that is.

I had dinner with someone quite a bit younger than me, but who also plays the role of a teacher to me. In different places, the two of us turned out to be thinking about the very same things. Somehow, the topic of AI keeps becoming the starting point for every train of thought. We both shared the sense that "a world unlike any before is beginning." Apparently, the most fun he's had lately was digging up sweet potatoes. And it turned out we'd both bought a pair of jeans for gardening around the same time.

Then the news came in about the earthquake in Kumamoto. Over and over, reports of new tremors kept coming. I imagine the anxiety is mounting, and I pray, with all my heart, that everyone is safe. Thank you, as always, for coming to Hobonichi today. It was a day that can't be tidied into a single shape. Then again, most days probably can't.
